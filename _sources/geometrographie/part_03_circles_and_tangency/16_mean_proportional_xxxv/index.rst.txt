Problem XXXV: Mean Proportional Between Two Segments
====================================================

.. rubric:: Émile Lemoine — *La Géométrographie* (English Translation, pp. 24–27, fols. ``0040–0043``)

.. folio:: 0040
   :page: p. 24

**XXXV.** — *Construct the mean proportional between two given straight lines* :math:`M` *and* :math:`N`, :math:`X^2 = M \cdot N`.

Let us first employ the two classical solutions, while making the drawing economies that our method suggests.

The first founded upon the proposition:
*In a right triangle, the perpendicular dropped from the vertex of the right angle onto the hypotenuse is the mean proportional between the two segments of the hypotenuse;*

The second upon:
*The length of the tangent drawn from a point* :math:`A` *to a circle is the mean proportional between the distances from point* :math:`A` *to the points* :math:`B` *and* :math:`C` *where a secant drawn through* :math:`A` *cuts the circle.*

*a)* I draw a line :math:`AB` on which I take :math:`AB = M`, :math:`BC = N` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (R_2 + 5C_1 + C_2 + 2C_3)`, let :math:`AB > BC`. I describe a circle on :math:`AC` as diameter, using the circumference :math:`A(M)` already drawn to obtain :math:`B` in order to find the midpoint :math:`O` of :math:`AC`, which saves :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (C_1 + C_3)`, leaving :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 3C_1 + 2C_3)`.

At point :math:`B`, I erect a perpendicular to :math:`AC` cutting :math:`O(OC)` at :math:`D`; I obtain it by the symbol :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 2C_1 + 2C_3)`, if I took care, while drawing :math:`B(N)` to place :math:`C`, to mark the second point :math:`C'` where :math:`B(N)` cuts :math:`AC`. :math:`DB` is the sought mean proportional.

:math:`\mathrm{Op.} : (4R_1 + 3R_2 + 10C_1 + C_2 + 6C_3)`; simplicity 24; exactitude 15; 3 lines, 6 circles.

*b)* I draw a line :math:`AB` on which I take :math:`AC = N`, :math:`AB = M` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (R_2 + 5C_1 + C_2 + 2C_3)`.

I describe on :math:`CB` as diameter a circumference, using the circumference :math:`A(M)` drawn to find :math:`B` in order to locate the midpoint :math:`O` of :math:`CB` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 3C_1 + 2C_3)`.

On :math:`AO` as diameter, I describe a circumference cutting :math:`O(OC)` at :math:`D` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 4C_1 + 3C_3)`.

.. folio:: 0041
   :page: p. 25

:math:`AD`, which need not be drawn, is the sought mean proportional.

:math:`\mathrm{Op.} : (4R_1 + 3R_2 + 12C_1 + C_2 + 7C_3)`; simplicity 27; exactitude 17; 3 lines, 7 circles.

NOTE. — If I employ two compasses, I can save :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (C_1 + C_3)` by using the circumference :math:`A(AB)` to find the midpoint of :math:`AO`, and one would have:

:math:`\mathrm{Op.} : (4R_1 + 3R_2 + 11C_1 + C_2 + 6C_3)`; simplicity 25; exactitude 16; 3 lines, 6 circles.

This shows that, *from the graphic point of view*, contrary to the observation generally made, the two classical solutions :math:`a` and :math:`b` are very nearly equivalent (see *Rouché* and *de Comberousse*, *Traité de Géométrie*, 1\ :sup:`re` partie, p. 152); both are moreover *very poor*, although we have simplified them by line economies. Here is the best I know:

*c)* Always let :math:`M` be the larger of the two lines :math:`M` and :math:`N`; I draw an arbitrary straight line :math:`AB` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (R_2)`.
I draw :math:`A(M)`, :math:`A` being any point on :math:`AB`, :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (2C_1 + C_2 + C_3)`.
:math:`A(M)` cuts :math:`AB` at :math:`B`; I draw :math:`B(N)` cutting :math:`BA` at :math:`C` between :math:`B` and :math:`A`; I draw :math:`C(N)` cutting :math:`B(N)` at :math:`P` and :math:`Q` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (4C_1 + 2C_3)`.
I draw :math:`PQ` cutting :math:`A(M)` at :math:`H` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2)`.
:math:`BH` is the sought line.

:math:`\mathrm{Op.} : (2R_1 + 2R_2 + 6C_1 + C_2 + 3C_3)`; simplicity 14; exactitude 9; 2 lines, 3 circles.

*c*\ :sub:`1`) One may also operate thus:
From an arbitrary point :math:`C` I draw :math:`C(M)`.
I draw an arbitrary radius :math:`CB` cutting :math:`C(M)` at :math:`B` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (R_1 + R_2 + 2C_1 + C_3)`.
I describe :math:`B(N)` cutting :math:`BC` at :math:`K`, between :math:`B` and :math:`C`; I draw :math:`K(N)` cutting :math:`B(N)` at :math:`P` and at :math:`Q` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (4C_1 + 2C_3)`.
I draw :math:`PQ` cutting :math:`C(M)` at :math:`A` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2)`.
:math:`AK` or :math:`AB` is the sought mean proportional, for the two isosceles triangles :math:`ACB`, :math:`BAK` are similar and have side :math:`AB` in common.

:math:`\mathrm{Op.} : (3R_1 + 2R_2 + 6C_1 + 3C_3)`; simplicity 14; exactitude 9; 2 lines, 3 circles.

One cannot say that this method of constructing a mean proportional is fundamentally new, for, save for a very slight graphic modification giving 14 instead of 15 as simplicity, it is found (*N. A.*, 1857, p. 125) under the name of M. :person:`Edm.-Aug. Gouzy <gouzy>`, of Lausanne, yet stated without commentary to bring out its extreme simplicity.

.. folio:: 0042
   :page: p. 26

Its symbol, executing the operation in the order indicated by M. *Gouzy*'s statement, is :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (R_2 + 8C_1 + C_2 + 5C_3)`, which is half of what the classical operation executed as ordinarily done would be, without the simplifications we have made, suggested by the systematic idea of simplification. 
Moreover, one had no positive criterion of simplicity; for some years this construction has been noted in teaching journals and several professors have shown it in their courses, yet without saying that it ought to *replace* constructions :math:`a` and :math:`b`.

I am not familiar with the methods of graphic statics, 
but I believe that the theory of Simplicity and Exactitude of constructions will find broad application there. (This is also M. *Gouzy*'s construction, indicated in M. A. *Favaro*'s *Leçons de Statique graphique*, *Terrier* translation, second part, p. 68, 1885.)

Here are two other simple solutions — less simple, however, than :math:`c` or :math:`c_1` — of the same problem:

*d)* I draw (*fig. 6*) an arbitrary circle of radius :g:`OB` such that :math:`OB > \frac{M}{2}` and draw in it the chord :g:`BC` equal to :math:`M` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 2C_1 + C_2 + 2C_3)`.

On :g:`BC` I take :math:`BK = N`; :g:`K` being between :g:`B` and :g:`C` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (3C_1 + C_3)`.

From :g:`K` I drop a perpendicular onto :g:`OB`, without :g:`OB` being traced, using the circle :g:`B(N)` already drawn to obtain :g:`K` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 2C_1 + C_3)`.

This perpendicular cuts the circle :g:`O(OB)` at :g:`A`.
:g:`AB` is the sought mean proportional.

:math:`\mathrm{Op.} : (4R_1 + 2R_2 + 7C_1 + C_2 + 4C_3)`; simplicity 18; exactitude 12; 2 lines, 4 circles.

*d*\ :sub:`1`) One can also draw :math:`BK = N` as a chord of a circle of sufficient radius and any center :math:`O` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 2C_1 + 2C_3)`.

Then take :math:`BC = M`; :math:`C` being on :math:`BK` in the direction :math:`BK` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (3C_1 + C_3)`.

Then from :math:`C` drop, without drawing :math:`OB`, a perpendicular onto :math:`OB` cutting circle :math:`O(OB)` at :math:`A` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : 2R_1 + R_2 + 2C_1 + C_3`.

:math:`AB` is the sought line because the two triangles :math:`BCA`, :math:`BKA` are similar and have side :math:`BA` in common.

*d*) and *d*\ :sub:`1`) have the same symbol.

*e*) I will also mention the elegant construction just given by M. *Léon Colette* (*Mathesis*, p. 192, 1892).

.. folio:: 0043
   :page: p. 27

I draw (*fig. 7*) an arbitrary circle :g:`O(OA)`, :math:`OA` being greater than :math:`M`.

From :g:`A`, an arbitrary point on this circle as center, I draw :math:`A(M)` cutting :g:`O(OA)` at :g:`B` and :g:`C`, then :math:`A(N)` cutting :g:`O(OA)` at :g:`F` and :g:`G`; points :g:`F`, :g:`B`, :g:`C`, :g:`G` following in that order, :math:`F, B, C, G` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (5C_1 + C_2 + 3C_3)`.

Let us draw :g:`AB`, :g:`AC` meeting :math:`A(N)` at :g:`D` and :g:`E`; then :g:`DE` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (6R_1 + 3R_2)` meeting :g:`O(OA)` at :g:`M`; :g:`AM` is the sought mean.

:math:`\mathrm{Op.} : (6R_1 + R_2 + 5C_1 + C_2 + 3C_3)`; simplicity 18; exactitude 12; 3 lines, 3 circles.
