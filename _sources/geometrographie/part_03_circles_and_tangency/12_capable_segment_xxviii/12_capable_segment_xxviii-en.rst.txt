Problem XXVIII: Circular Segment Capable of a Given Angle
=========================================================

.. rubric:: Émile Lemoine — *La Géométrographie* (English Translation, p. 16, fol. ``0032``)

.. folio:: 0032
   :page: p. 16

**XXVIII.** — *To construct upon a given straight line* :g:`AB` *a segment capable of a given angle* :g:`EGD`.

The classical method, *carried out without useless lines*, gives:

:math:`\mathrm{Op.} : (6R_1 + 3R_2 + 10C_1 + 7C_3)` ; simplicity 26 ; exactitude 16 ; 3 lines, 7 circles.

Here is a simpler construction (*fig.* 2):

I trace :g:`A(AB)`, :g:`B(AB)` intersecting at :g:`P` and :g:`Q` . . . :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (3C_1 + 2C_3)`.

I trace :g:`C(AB)` cutting :g:`CD` at :g:`D`, :g:`CE` at :g:`E`; I take :g:`F` on :g:`C(AB)` such that arc :math:`EF` = arc :math:`ED` . . . :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (3C_1 + 2C_3)`.

I take :g:`D` on :g:`A(AB)` such that arc :math:`BPD` = arc :math:`DEF`; I trace :g:`BD`, :g:`PQ` intersecting at :g:`O`; I trace :g:`O(OA)` . . . :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (4R_1 + 2R_2 + 5C_1 + 2C_3)`, and we have the sought segment:

:math:`\mathrm{Op.} : (4R_1 + 2R_2 + 11C_1 + 6C_3)` ; simplicity 23 ; exactitude 15 ; 2 lines, 6 circles.
