Problems XXXVI–XXXVII: Golden Section & Inaccessible Intersections
==================================================================

.. rubric:: Émile Lemoine — *La Géométrographie* (English Translation, pp. 27–28, fols. ``0043–0044``)

.. folio:: 0043
   :page: p. 27

**XXXVI.** — *Divide a straight line* :g:`AB` *in extreme and mean ratio.*

*a)* By the classical method:

I take the midpoint :math:`\omega` of :g:`AB` and erect at :g:`B` a perpendicular to :g:`AB` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (4R_1 + 2R_2 + 4C_1 + 4C_3)`.

On the perpendicular to :g:`AB` drawn at :g:`B`, I take :math:`B\omega = BO` and draw :math:`\omega(BO)` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (3C_1 + 2C_3)`.

I draw :math:`A\omega` cutting :math:`\omega(BO)` in two points :math:`l` and :math:`m`, :math:`l` being between :math:`A` and :math:`\omega` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2)`.

I draw :math:`A(Al)` cutting :g:`AB` at :g:`M` between :g:`A` and :g:`B` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (2C_1 + C_3)`.

:math:`AM` and :math:`BM` are the sought segments.

:math:`\mathrm{Op.} : (6R_1 + 3R_2 + 9C_1 + 7C_3)`; simplicity 25; exactitude 15; 3 lines, [7] circles.

*b)* Here is a means indicated to me by General *Parmentier*, but its symbol is *somewhat* more complicated.

I erect at :g:`B` a perpendicular to :g:`AB` and take on it :math:`BC = 2AB`; the bisector of angle :math:`CAB` cuts :math:`BC` at :math:`D`; I take on :math:`BA`, :math:`BM = BD`; :g:`M` is the sought point.

*c)* The following construction is the simplest I know; it rests upon this theorem: *If the length of the tangent drawn from point* :g:`M` *to a circle equals the length of a chord* :g:`AB` *of that circle, a chord passing through* :g:`M`, :math:`MA` *and* :math:`MB` *are the greater segments (additive or subtractive) of* :g:`AB` *divided in extreme and mean ratio (* :math:`M, B, A` *following in that order).*

I describe (*fig. 8*) :g:`A(AB)`, :g:`B(AB)` intersecting at :g:`C` and :g:`C'`; I describe :g:`C(AB)` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (4C_1 + 3C_3)`.

.. folio:: 0044
   :page: p. 28

I draw through :g:`A` the tangent to :g:`C(AB)`; for this I describe :g:`C'(AB)` cutting :g:`B(AB)` at :g:`D` and draw :g:`AD` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + C_1 + C_3)`.

This is the sought tangent; it cuts :g:`A(AB)` at :g:`E`.

I describe :g:`C(CE)` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (2C_1 + C_3)` cutting :math:`BA` at :g:`M`. 
Since the tangent drawn from :g:`M` to :g:`C(AB)` has the same length as :math:`AE`, and consequently as :g:`AB`, :math:`AM` is the length of the greater segment of :g:`AB` divided additively in extreme and mean ratio.

I therefore describe :math:`A(AM)`, which gives me on :g:`AB` the sought division point :g:`P` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (2C_1 + C_3)`.

In all: :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 6C_1 + 6C_3)`; simplicity 18; exactitude 11; 1 line, 6 circles.

REMARK. — This construction is much simpler than the classical construction; yet it may seem, on looking at *figure 8*, to be more complicated; 
this appearance arises from the fact that, in *figure 8*, we have drawn *all the lines* used, whereas for the classical figure, to which one is accustomed, one simply says: at :g:`B` I draw a perpendicular to :g:`AB`, I lay off on this perpendicular a length equal to half of :g:`AB`, etc., 
but none of the auxiliary lines necessary for these operations are drawn on the figure; if one draws them all, the greater complication of the classical procedure leaps immediately to the eye; a similar remark would apply to nearly all the questions we treat in this memoir.

**XXXVII.** — *Draw through a point* :math:`P` *a straight line passing through the point of intersection of two given straight lines that cannot be extended thither.*

This problem has received a very large number of solutions. Here is the one whose symbol is the simplest among those I know:

Let :math:`AA'A''`, :math:`BB'B''` be the two given straight lines:

I draw two arbitrary straight lines :math:`A'B'`, :math:`A''B''` intersecting at :math:`I`, then another arbitrary straight line :math:`IAB` passing through :math:`I` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (R_1 + 3R_2)`.

I draw :math:`PA'` and :math:`PB'` cutting :math:`AIB` at :math:`E` and :math:`F` respectively; then :math:`A''E` and :math:`B''F` intersecting at :math:`P'` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (8R_1 + 4R_2)`.

I draw :math:`PP'`, which is the sought straight line :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2)`.

:math:`\mathrm{Op.} : (11R_1 + 8R_2)`; simplicity 19; exactitude 11; 8 lines.
