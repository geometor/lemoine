Introduction
============

.. folio:: 0017
   :page: p. 1

**FRENCH ASSOCIATION FOR THE ADVANCEMENT OF SCIENCE** — *Congress of Pau, 1892. Session of 16 September 1892.*

**M. Émile LEMOINE**, *Former Student of the École Polytechnique, Paris.* — **GEOMETROGRAPHY, OR THE ART OF GEOMETRIC CONSTRUCTIONS**

The memoir which we present to the First Section may appear, at first glance, to contain part of the results we already gave in 1888 at the Congress of Oran; yet, in the parts sharing a common subject, it is neither the reproduction nor even the complement of that memoir; it *corrects* it, and nevertheless the Oran memoir is *exact* from the point of view we were then adopting. Indeed, we had just conceived the general idea of measuring simplicity in the mathematical sciences—both in reasonings and in constructions; we had developed its application to evaluating the simplicity of constructions executed with straightedge and compass, starting from the time-honored classical constructions adopted as fundamental constructions, and we had applied our method to evaluating their simplicity so that one might adopt the symbols of those constructions to evaluate the simplicity of the solutions of any problem whatever. That aim we fulfilled as regards the classical solutions examined. We were far from suspecting that those fundamental constructions were,

.. _lemoine-en-ideal-postulates:

.. folio:: 0018
   :page: p. 2

so to speak, *all* in need of reform and reduction—even the simplest of them, such as *drawing through a given point a parallel to a given straight line*—so that they must be taken up anew in order to give a real foundation to the applications of our theory. It is this study that we undertake today, adding to it the notion—more important still than that of *simplicity*—of the *exactitude* of constructions. In the Oran memoir, several of the *applied* constructions—such as, for example, *drawing the bisector of an angle whose sides cannot be produced to the vertex*—are not the simplest, and it is the simplest that I ought to have sought; but I was not yet accustomed to handling the method, which is far more delicate to apply without error than the *extreme* simplicity of its exposition might lead one to suppose. I instinctively took as standard types the constructions simplest to *express* as being the simplest to draw, without yet having noticed that there was *no connection* between that simplicity of expression and real simplicity of execution. Apart from these remarks, everything of a general character in the Oran memoir remains exact, and we refer the reader to it for such generalities expressed there as we may not have repeated here.
