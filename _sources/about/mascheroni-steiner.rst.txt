Mascheroni (1797) & Poncelet–Steiner (1833)
===========================================

Euclidean geometry traditionally postulates *both* the ungraduated straightedge (Postulates 1–2) and the compass (Postulate 3). Between 1672 and 1890, synthetic and projective geometers discovered that either instrument can dispense with the other—provided a subtle asymmetry between circles and lines is respected:

1. **Compass Alone — Georg Mohr (1672) & Lorenzo Mascheroni (1797)**:
   First proved by Georg Mohr in *Euclides Danicus* (Amsterdam, 1672) and independently developed into a twelve-book constructive masterpiece by **Lorenzo Mascheroni** in *La Geometria del Compasso* (Pavia, 1797), every Euclidean point-determination problem can be solved using **only the compass** (:math:`m_1 C_1 + m_2 C_2 + m_3 C_3`). Straight lines cannot be continuously drawn, but any line is completely determined by two of its points :math:`(A, B)`, and circular inversion (:math:`5C_1 + 3C_3`,August Adler, 1890) maps lines and circles into circles.

2. **Straightedge Alone with One Fixed Circle — Poncelet (1822) & Jakob Steiner (1833)**:
   Conversely, a straightedge *alone* in an empty plane can execute only projectively invariant constructions (such as harmonic conjugates and polars) and cannot solve quadratic metric problems. **Jean-Victor Poncelet** (1822) observed, and **Jakob Steiner** rigorously demonstrated in *Die geometrischen Constructionen, ausgeführt mittelst der geraden Linie und Eines festen Kreises* (Berlin, 1833), that **a single permanently drawn circle together with its center** :math:`M` suffices to execute *every* ruler-and-compass construction using the straightedge alone (:math:`l_1 R_1 + l_2 R_2`) via eight canonical problems (§ 18, ``[68]``–``[85]``) built from complete quadrangles, centers of similitude (*Aehnlichkeitspunkte*), and radical axes (*Linie der gleichen Potenzen*).
