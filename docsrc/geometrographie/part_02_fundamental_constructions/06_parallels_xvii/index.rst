Problem XVII: Parallel Through an External Point
================================================

.. rubric:: Émile Lemoine — *La Géométrographie* (English Translation, pp. 9–10, fols. ``0025–0026``)

.. folio:: 0025
   :page: p. 9

**XVII.** — *Through a point* :g:`A` *taken outside a straight line* :g:`BC`, *to draw a parallel to that straight line.*

The classical method gives:

:math:`\mathrm{Op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 5C_1 + 3C_3)`; simplicity 11; exactitude 7; 1 straight line, 3 circles [*]_ .

But here are two which give simpler results and which were pointed out to me by M. *Tarry (Gaston)*:

1° Through :g:`A` I pass a circle cutting :g:`BC` at :g:`B` and at :g:`C` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (C_1 + C_3)`.

I take :g:`BA` and I trace the circle :g:`C(BA)` which cuts the first circle at :g:`D` and I join :g:`AD` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 3C_1 + C_3)`.

Symbol: :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 4C_1 + 2C_3)`; simplicity 9; exactitude 6; 1 straight line, 2 circles.

2° I construct a rhombus :g:`ABCD`:

:math:`\mathrm{Op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 3C_1 + 3C_3)`; simplicity 9; exactitude 5; 1 straight line, 3 circles.

REMARK. — These simplifications are important on account of the frequency of this construction in drafting plates.

3° *Case where the straight line* :g:`BC`, *not drawn, is given by two points* :g:`B` *and* :g:`C`.

I take :g:`BC`, I trace :g:`A(BC)` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (3C_1 + C_3)`.

I take :g:`AB`, I trace :g:`C(AB)` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (3C_1 + C_3)`.

These two circles cut at :g:`D`, I trace :g:`AD` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2)`.

Symbol: :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 6C_1 + 2C_3)`; simplicity 11; exactitude 8; 1 straight line, 2 circles.

.. [*] I take this opportunity to make a remark not directly relating to our subject. It is known that the construction is performed thus: one describes a circle :g:`C(CB)`, a circle :g:`B(CB)`, a circle :g:`B(AC)` which cuts :g:`C(CB)` in two points :g:`D` and :g:`D'`; :g:`CD` is parallel to :g:`AB`.

   I sought the locus of :g:`D'` when the radius varies. One immediately finds that it has for equation: :math:`\rho = \frac{l}{\sin\frac{\omega}{2}}`. :g:`C` being the pole, :g:`CD` the polar axis, :math:`l` the distance from :g:`C` to :g:`AB`.
