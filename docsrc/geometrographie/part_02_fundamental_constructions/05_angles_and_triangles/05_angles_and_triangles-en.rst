Problems XI–XVI: Copying Angles & Constructing Triangles
========================================================

.. rubric:: Émile Lemoine — *La Géométrographie* (English Translation, pp. 7–9, fols. ``0023–0025``)

.. folio:: 0023
   :page: p. 7

In Book I of :person:`Euclid <euclid>`'s *Elements* (:cite:`euclid-heath`; see :external+euclid:doc:`index` and :external+elements:doc:`index`), Proposition I.23 transfers a rectilinear angle by joining two arbitrary points across its arms and invoking Proposition I.22 (triangle from three segments)—drawing an unnecessary auxiliary chord (:math:`4R_1 + 2R_2 + 8C_1 + C_2 + 4C_3`, :math:`S = 19, E = 13`). Lemoine replaces this speculative detour with direct equal-radius arc transfer and proceeds systematically through the five classical triangle constructions:

**Problem XI (Euclid I.23 — Equal Angle :math:`\angle DAE` at Given Point :math:`B` on Traced Line :math:`BC`).**
Sweep :math:`A(AE)` of arbitrary radius cutting :math:`AD` at :math:`D` and :math:`AC` at :math:`E` (:math:`C_1 + C_3`), then with the *same* unaltered radius sweep :math:`B(AE)` cutting :math:`BC` at :math:`F` (:math:`C_1 + C_3`). Capture chord :math:`DE` and sweep :math:`F(DE)` cutting :math:`B(AE)` at :math:`H` (:math:`3C_1 + C_3`), and draw :math:`BH` (:math:`2R_1 + R_2`):

.. math::

   \mathrm{Op.} : \left(2R_1 + R_2 + 5C_1 + 3C_3\right), \qquad S = 11, \quad E = 7 \quad (1\text{ line}, 3\text{ circles}).

**Problem XII — Third Angle :math:`\gamma` of a Triangle Given Angles :math:`\alpha` and :math:`\beta`.**
Trace an arbitrary straight line :math:`AB` (:math:`R_2`). With one shared arbitrary radius :math:`R`, sweep three circles :math:`\alpha(R)`, :math:`\beta(R)`, and :math:`O(R)` centered at an indeterminate point :math:`O` on :math:`AB` cutting :math:`AB` at :math:`B` (:math:`2C_1 + C_2 + 3C_3`). Step the chord of :math:`\alpha` from :math:`B` to :math:`E` on :math:`O(R)` (:math:`3C_1 + C_3`), then step the chord of :math:`\beta` from :math:`E` to :math:`H` (:math:`3C_1 + C_3`), and draw :math:`OH` (:math:`2R_1 + R_2`). The supplementary angle :math:`\angle HOA` equals :math:`\gamma` without ever drawing the intermediate ray :math:`OE` (saving :math:`2R_1 + R_2 + 2C_1` over Lemoine's 1888 Oran evaluation):

.. math::

   \mathrm{Op.} : \left(2R_1 + 2R_2 + 8C_1 + C_2 + 5C_3\right) \quad [\text{printed } 6C_3\text{ on p. 7}], \qquad S = 18, \quad E = 10.

**Problem XIII — Triangle Given Side :math:`a` and Adjacent Angles :math:`\angle xoy, \angle x'o'y'`.**
Trace an arbitrary line :math:`BC` and lay off :math:`BC = a` from indeterminate :math:`B` (:math:`R_2 + 2C_1 + C_2 + C_3`), retaining radius :math:`BC` to sweep :math:`o(BC)`, :math:`o'(BC)`, and :math:`C(BC)` (:math:`3C_1 + 3C_3`). Transfer chords :math:`xy` to :math:`C'` on :math:`B(BC)` and :math:`x'y'` to :math:`B'` on :math:`C(BC)` (:math:`6C_1 + 2C_3`), and draw :math:`CB', BC'` meeting at :math:`A` (:math:`4R_1 + 2R_2`): :math:`4R_1 + 3R_2 + 11C_1 + C_2 + 6C_3` (:math:`S = 25, E = 16`, 3 lines, 6 circles). Operating directly on the given side reduces this to :math:`4R_1 + 2R_2 + 10C_1 + 6C_3` (:math:`S = 22`), or on one given angle to :math:`2R_1 + R_2 + 8C_1 + 4C_3` (:math:`S = 15`).

**Problem XIV — Triangle Given Two Sides :math:`b, c` and Included Angle :math:`\angle xoy`.**

.. folio:: 0025
   :page: p. 9

Draw an arbitrary line (:math:`R_2`), lay off :math:`AC = b` from indeterminate :math:`A` (:math:`2C_1 + C_2 + C_3`), sweep :math:`o(AC)` (:math:`C_1 + C_3`), transfer chord :math:`xy` to :math:`B'` on :math:`A(AC)` (:math:`3C_1 + C_3`), draw :math:`AB'` (:math:`2R_1 + R_2`), lay off :math:`AB = c` on :math:`AB'` (:math:`3C_1 + C_3`), and join :math:`CB` (:math:`2R_1 + R_2`): :math:`4R_1 + 3R_2 + 9C_1 + C_2 + 4C_3` (:math:`S = 21, E = 14`, 3 lines, 4 circles).

**Problem XV — Triangle Given Two Sides :math:`a, b` and Opposite Angle :math:`B`.**
When both ambiguous-case solutions exist: :math:`6R_1 + 4R_2 + 9C_1 + C_2 + 4C_3` (:math:`S = 24, E = 16`, 4 lines, 4 circles).

**Problem XVI (Euclid I.22 — Triangle from Three Given Sides :math:`a, b, c`).**
Drawing the initial base carrier as an arbitrary line (:math:`R_2`) rather than joining two pre-placed points saves three operations over textbook execution (:math:`S = 22`, :ref:`concordance-triangle-sss`):

.. math::

   \mathrm{Op.} : \left(4R_1 + 3R_2 + 8C_1 + C_2 + 3C_3\right), \qquad S = 19, \quad E = 13 \quad (3\text{ lines}, 3\text{ circles}).
