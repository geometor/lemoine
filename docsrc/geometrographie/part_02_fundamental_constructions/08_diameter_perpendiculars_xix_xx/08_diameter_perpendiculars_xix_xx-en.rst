Problems XIX–XX: Circle Center & Perpendiculars
===============================================

.. rubric:: Émile Lemoine — *La Géométrographie* (English Translation, pp. 10–12, fols. ``0026–0028``)

.. folio:: 0026
   :page: p. 10

**XIX.** — *To describe a circle upon a given straight line* :g:`AB` *as diameter.*

One takes the midpoint :g:`O` of :g:`AB` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 2C_1 + 2C_3)`.

One takes the length :g:`OA`, then describes :g:`O(OA)` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (2C_1 + C_3)`.

Symbol: :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 4C_1 + 3C_3)`; simplicity 10; exactitude 6; 1 straight line, 3 circles.

**XX.** — *To trace through a point* :g:`C` *a perpendicular to a straight line* :g:`AB`.

1° The point :g:`C` is outside the straight line.

*Classical method.*

*a)* Symbol: :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 3C_1 + 3C_3)`; simplicity 9; exactitude 5; 1 straight line, 3 circles.

.. folio:: 0027
   :page: p. 11

*Another method.*

*b)* :g:`B` being any point of :g:`AB`, I describe :g:`B(BC)` which cuts :g:`AB` at :g:`A` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (C_2 + C_1 + C_3)`.

I take :g:`AC` and describe :g:`A(AC)` which cuts :g:`B(BC)` at :g:`C'`; I trace :g:`CC'` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 2C_1 + C_3)`.

Total symbol: :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 3C_1 + C_2 + 2C_3)`; simplicity 9; exactitude 6; 1 straight line, 2 circles.

2° The point :g:`C` is on :g:`AB`.

*Classical method.*

*a)* Same symbol and same elementary operations as when :g:`C` is outside the line; the following method is slightly simpler.

*b)* I place one leg at an arbitrary point :g:`O` outside :g:`AB`; I place the other leg at :g:`C` and describe the circumference :g:`O(OC)` which also cuts :g:`AB` at :g:`A`; I trace :g:`AO` which cuts :g:`O(OC)` at :g:`C'` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + C_1 + C_3)`.

I trace :g:`C'C` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2)`.

Symbol: :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (4R_1 + 2R_2 + C_1 + C_3)`; simplicity 8; exactitude 5; 2 straight lines, 1 circle.

REMARK. — This method *b* which is classically given for the case where the straight line :g:`AB` cannot be produced beyond :g:`A` is simpler than the general classical method *a* given when :g:`C` is arbitrary on :g:`AB`; *b* must therefore always be employed, and there is no need to separate the case where :g:`C` falls at :g:`A`,

:g:`A` being the extremity of :g:`AB` when that position is imposed by the dimensions of the drawing.

If one wishes to *erect an arbitrary perpendicular* to :g:`AB`, one then has:

Symbol: :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (4R_1 + 2R_2 + C_3)`; simplicity 7; exactitude 4; 2 straight lines, 1 circle.

One may also, through :g:`A` and :g:`B`, arbitrary points of :g:`AB`, trace two arbitrary circles; they intersect along a perpendicular to :g:`AB`.

:math:`\mathrm{Op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 2C_2 + 2C_3)`; simplicity 7; exactitude 4; 1 straight line, 2 circles.

If one wishes to *erect an arbitrary perpendicular to a straight line (not drawn) given by two points* :g:`A` *and* :g:`B`, one describes :g:`A(R)`, :g:`B(R')`; :g:`R` and :g:`R'` being arbitrary, the intersection of these two circles solves the question.

:math:`\mathrm{Op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 2C_1 + 2C_3)`; simplicity 7; exactitude 4; 1 straight line, 2 circles.

*To drop from a point* :g:`C` *external to a straight line (not drawn) given by two points* :g:`A` *and* :g:`B`, *a perpendicular upon its direction.*

One draws :g:`A(AC)`, :g:`B(BC)`; the intersection of these two circles is the required perpendicular.

.. folio:: 0028
   :page: p. 12

:math:`\mathrm{Op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 4C_1 + 2C_3)`; simplicity 9; exactitude 6; 1 straight line, 2 circles.

REMARK I. — If one wishes to *draw the perpendicular at* :g:`A` *to a straight line* :g:`AB` *not drawn and given by two points* :g:`A` *and* :g:`B`, one must proceed thus:

Trace :g:`B(R)`, :g:`A(R)` with a same arbitrary radius :g:`R` intersecting at :g:`C`, then :g:`C(R)` passing through :g:`A` and :g:`B`, trace :g:`BC` cutting :g:`C(R)` at :g:`C'`, and trace :g:`AC'`.

:math:`\mathrm{Op.} : (4R_1 + 2R_2 + 3C_1 + 3C_3)`; simplicity 12; exactitude 7; 2 straight lines, 3 circles.

It is rather curious to remark that when the straight line is given by two points :g:`A` and :g:`B`, it is simpler to draw a perpendicular to it through an arbitrary point than through one of the given points.

REMARK II. — When, in a construction, one has to erect perpendiculars at :math:`n` given points :g:`A, B, C, D...`

of given straight lines :g:`L, M, N, P...`, there is an advantage, if :math:`n > 5`, in operating thus:

I draw a first perpendicular at :g:`A` to :g:`M` by an operation whose simplicity is 8 (see XX, 2° *b*).

I describe from all the given points as centers circumferences of the same radius; simplicity: :math:`2n`.

I take upon the circumference traced at :g:`A` the chord of the quadrant; simplicity: 2.

I transfer it to all the other circumferences and, by their means, I trace the perpendiculars; simplicity: :math:`5(n - 1)`.

Therefore they will be traced by an operation of simplicity :math:`5 + 7n`, instead of :math:`8n` which the general construction would give. There will therefore be an advantage in taking it if:

.. math::

   5 + 7n < 8n \quad \text{or} \quad 5 < n.
