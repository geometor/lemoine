Problems XXXVIII–XLVI: Inversion, Polarity, Radical Axis & Harmonic Conjugates
==============================================================================

.. rubric:: Émile Lemoine — *La Géométrographie* (English Translation, pp. 29–33, fols. ``0045–0049``)

.. folio:: 0045
   :page: p. 29

**XXXVIII.** — *Locate the point* :math:`A'` *reciprocal to a given point* :math:`A` *with respect to a given circle of radius* :math:`R` *and center* :math:`O`.

Two cases to examine:

1° :math:`OA > \frac{R}{2}`.

I draw :math:`A(AO)` cutting :math:`O(R)` at :math:`B` and :math:`C`.
I draw :math:`B(R)`, :math:`C(R)` intersecting at :math:`O` and :math:`A` [i.e., :math:`A'`].
:math:`A'` is the sought point.

:math:`\mathrm{Op.} : (5C_1 + 3C_3)`; simplicity 8; exactitude 5; 3 circles.

2° :math:`OA < \frac{R}{2}`.

I draw :math:`OA`; I draw :math:`A(R)` cutting :math:`O(R)` at :math:`B` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 3C_1 + C_3)`.

I draw :math:`B(R)` cutting :math:`O(R)` at :math:`D` and :math:`D'`; I draw :math:`DD'` cutting :math:`OA` at :math:`A'` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + C_1 + C_3)`.

In all: :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (4R_1 + 2R_2 + 4C_1 + 2C_3)`; simplicity 12; exactitude 8; 2 circles, 2 lines.

Thus, in seeking the general symbol of a construction, it is this latter symbol that must be adopted to count the search for the reciprocal of a point :math:`A` with respect to a circle of radius :math:`R`, if it does not follow from the general data that :math:`OA > \frac{R}{2}`.

Here is a construction that also applies whatever :math:`A` may be, though it is *somewhat* more complicated.

I draw a circle with center :math:`A` cutting the given circle at :math:`B` and :math:`C`.

I draw :math:`AB`, :math:`AC` cutting the given circle at :math:`B'` and :math:`C'` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (4R_1 + 2R_2 + C_1 + C_3)`.

I draw :math:`B'C`, :math:`C'B` intersecting at :math:`A'` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (4R_1 + 2R_2)`.

:math:`\mathrm{Op.} : (8R_1 + 4R_2 + C_1 + C_3)`; simplicity 14; exactitude 9; 4 lines, 1 circle.

**XXXIX.** — *Draw the polar of a point* :math:`A` *with respect to a circumference of center* :math:`O` *and radius* :math:`R`.

*a)* Through :math:`A` I draw two arbitrary straight lines: the first cutting the circumference at :math:`B` and :math:`B'`, the second at :math:`C` and :math:`C'` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + 2R_2)`.

I draw :math:`B'C'` and :math:`BC` intersecting at :math:`D`, and :math:`BC'`, :math:`CB'` intersecting at :math:`E`.

I draw :math:`ED`; this is the sought polar.

:math:`\mathrm{Op.} : (12R_1 + 7R_2)`; simplicity 19; exactitude 12; 7 lines.

.. folio:: 0046
   :page: p. 30

*b)* I can also, :math:`D` being marked as before, drop a perpendicular from :math:`D` onto :math:`OA` (without drawing :math:`OA`) :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 4C_1 + 2C_3)`.

In all: :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (8R_1 + 5R_2 + 4C_1 + 2C_3)`; simplicity 19; exactitude 12; 5 lines, 2 circles.

I have not been able to find a *general* construction of the polar of a given point :math:`A` with respect to a circle simpler than these two.

It is by error that I give 15 as Simplicity in my note in *Mathesis*, 1888, page 222. I had not noticed the case where :math:`OA < \frac{R}{2}`.

There are a great many *particular* constructions of the same problem.

*c)* *Non-general* construction applicable in the case where :math:`OA > \frac{R}{2}`.

I describe :math:`A(OA)` cutting the given circle at :math:`B` and :math:`C`.

I describe :math:`B(R)`, :math:`C(R)` intersecting at :math:`A'` reciprocal of :math:`A` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (5C_1 + 3C_3)`.

I draw :math:`OC` cutting :math:`C(R)` at :math:`D`; I draw :math:`DA'` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (4R_1 + 2R_2)`; this is the sought polar.

:math:`\mathrm{Op.} : (4R_1 + 2R_2 + 5C_1 + 3C_3)`; simplicity 14; exactitude 9; 2 lines, 3 circles.

If :math:`A` is exterior to the given circle, one may also draw a circle on :math:`OA` as diameter; the intersection of the two circles is the sought polar; the symbol is then:

:math:`\mathrm{Op.} : (6R_1 + 3R_2 + 4C_1 + 3C_3)`; simplicity 16; exactitude 10; 3 lines, 3 circles.

**XL.** — *Locate the pole* :math:`L` *of a straight line* :math:`XY` *with respect to a given circumference of center* :math:`O` *and radius* :math:`R`.

Two cases to consider:

1° :math:`XY` cuts circle :math:`O(R)` at :math:`M` and :math:`N`.

I draw the tangent at :math:`M` to circle :math:`O(R)` (see construction XXV) :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 4C_1 + 3C_3)`.

I draw circle :math:`N(R)` cutting at :math:`O'` the circle :math:`M(R)` drawn to obtain the tangent at :math:`M` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (C_1 + C_3)`.

I draw :math:`OO'` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2)`; :math:`OO'` cuts the tangent at :math:`M` at the sought pole :math:`L`.

:math:`\mathrm{Op.} : (4R_1 + 2R_2 + 5C_1 + 4C_3)`; simplicity 15; exactitude 9; 2 lines, 4 circles.

2° :math:`XY` does not cut circle :math:`O(R)`. (This solution applies even if :math:`XY` cuts circle :math:`O(R)`, provided the distance from :math:`O` to :math:`XY` is greater than :math:`\frac{R}{2}`).

.. folio:: 0047
   :page: p. 31

From :math:`O` I drop onto :math:`XY` a perpendicular whose foot on :math:`XY` is :math:`F` and which cuts :math:`O(R)` at :math:`K` on the same side of :math:`O` as :math:`F` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 3C_1 + 3C_3)`.

I describe :math:`F(FO)` cutting :math:`O(R)` at :math:`H`; I describe :math:`H(R)` cutting :math:`OF` at :math:`L` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (4C_1 + 2C_3)`.

:math:`L` is the sought pole; for the two similar isosceles triangles :math:`OFH`, :math:`OHL` have side :math:`OH` in common, hence :math:`\overline{OH}^2` or :math:`R^2 = OL \cdot OF`.

:math:`\mathrm{Op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 7C_1 + 5C_3)`; simplicity 15; exactitude 9; 1 line, 5 circles.

These two cases together form a general construction of simplicity 15, for if one is not applicable, the other is.

There are still a great many general constructions for the same problem, yet I know none as simple as the two I give here.

**XLI.** — *Draw the radical axis of two given circumferences* :math:`O(R)`, :math:`O'(R')`.

I draw two circumferences :math:`\omega(\rho)`, :math:`\omega'(\rho')` each cutting both given circumferences, etc.

:math:`\mathrm{Op.} : (10R_1 + 5R_2 + 2C_3)`; simplicity 17; exactitude 10; 5 lines, 2 circles.

If the circumferences are tangent, the symbol reduces to that of the tangent at the point of contact :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 4C_1 + 3C_3)`.

If they intersect, to :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2)`.

**XLII.** — *Locate the radical center of three circumferences* :math:`R_1, R_2, R_3`.

*a)* :math:`R_1, R_2, R_3` are exterior to one another, or else one of them, :math:`R_3`, for example, is tangent to only one of the first two.

One draws the two circumferences :math:`\omega(\rho)`, :math:`\omega'(\rho')` of the preceding problem so that they cut all three given circumferences; one finds:

:math:`\mathrm{Op.} : (16R_1 + 8R_2 + 2C_3)`.

*b)* :math:`R_1` and :math:`R_2` are exterior and :math:`R_3` touches :math:`R_1` and :math:`R_2`, or they touch two by two.

I draw :math:`\omega(\rho)` only; by means of :math:`\omega(\rho)`, I construct a point :math:`K_1` of the radical axis of :math:`R_1` and :math:`R_3`; I join :math:`K_1` to the point of contact :math:`L_1` of :math:`R_1` and :math:`R_3`, :math:`L_1` being located by drawing :math:`O_1 O_3` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (8R_1 + 4R_2 + C_3)`.

Likewise, I construct a point :math:`K_2` of the radical axis of :math:`R_2` and :math:`R_3` and join :math:`K_2` to the point of contact :math:`L_2` of :math:`R_2` and :math:`R_3` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (6R_1 + 3R_2)`.

In all: :math:`\mathrm{Op.} : (16R_1 + 8R_2 + C_3)`.

*c)* :math:`R_1, R_2` intersect, :math:`R_3` is exterior.

.. folio:: 0048
   :page: p. 32

I draw :math:`\omega(\rho)`, :math:`\omega'(\rho')`; I determine the radical axis of :math:`R_1` and :math:`R_3` or of :math:`R_2` and :math:`R_3`, which cuts the intersection of :math:`R_1` and :math:`R_2` at the sought point.

:math:`\mathrm{Op.} : (16R_1 + 8R_2 + 2C_3)`.

*d)* :math:`R_1, R_2` intersect, :math:`R_3` touches one of the first two or both.

I draw only :math:`\omega(\rho)` and determine with this circumference the radical axis of two tangent circumferences:

:math:`\mathrm{Op.} : (8R_1 + 4R_2 + C_3)`.

*e)* :math:`R_1, R_2, R_3` intersect two by two:

:math:`\mathrm{Op.} : (4R_1 + 2R_2)`.

**XLIII.** — *Locate a point* :math:`M` *given by its Cartesian coordinates* :math:`x, y` *relative to two given axes* :math:`ox, oy`.

I take :math:`OA = x` on the :math:`x`-axis :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (3C_1 + C_3)`.
I take :math:`OB = y` on the :math:`y`-axis :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (3C_1 + C_3)`.
I describe :math:`A(y)` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (C_1 + C_3)`.
Then, taking :math:`x` again between the compass legs, I describe :math:`B(x)` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (3C_1 + C_3)`.
:math:`A(y)` and :math:`B(x)` intersect at :math:`M`:

:math:`\mathrm{Op.} : (10C_1 + 4C_3)`; simplicity 14; exactitude 10; 4 circles.

If I use two compasses, I do not need to take :math:`x` again, but rather use the first compass; I thus save :math:`2C_1` and have only:

:math:`\mathrm{Op.} : (8C_1 + 4C_3)` [*]_ .

.. [*] This question is one of those I have already treated (Congress of Oran, 1888, p. 92, construction XXXI, and *Bulletin de la Soc. math. de France*, vol. XVI, 1887–88, p. 163);
   yet, simple as it is, I had given too complicated a symbol, because I had adopted another usual construction, just as simple to express as this one; my attention not then being fixed as it is now on the differences existing among the various fundamental constructions,
   I had chosen and evaluated the first classical construction that came to mind, regarding it without examination as equivalent to the others; there are similar errors in many of the constructions I have given hitherto. Those in this memoir are the simplest *that I have been able to find*,
   but they are fixed as the simplest in fact only until geometers find preferable ones. It is a small experimental piece of work that will be done very quickly by one person or another, if the question holds interest.
   Two years ago, I had a rather long correspondence on this subject with M. :person:`G. Tarry <tarry>`, and I take this opportunity to thank him, for a great number of the simplifications I have made here were pointed out to me by him in that correspondence.

**XLIV.** — *Locate the centers of similitude* :math:`V` *and* :math:`V_1` *of two circumferences* :math:`O(R)`, :math:`O'(R')`.

Referring back to construction XXIX (second method), we see that these points are determined by the symbol:

.. folio:: 0049
   :page: p. 33

:math:`\mathrm{Op.} : (6R_1 + 3R_2 + 4C_1 + 2C_3)`; simplicity 15; exactitude 10; 3 lines, 2 circles.

A single one of the two centers would be determined by:

:math:`\mathrm{Op.} : (4R_1 + 2R_2 + 4C_1 + 2C_3)`.

**XLV.** — *Draw the four axes of similitude of three given circumferences* :math:`O(R), O'(R'), O''(R'')`.

*a)* By determining the centers of similitude through the preceding construction, noting that one need only locate the four centers of similitude of :math:`O(R)` and :math:`O''(R'')`, of :math:`O'(R')` and :math:`O''(R'')`, and that :math:`O'O` need not be drawn, one has the symbol:

:math:`\mathrm{Op.} : (20R_1 + 10R_2 + 10C_1 + 6C_3)`; simplicity 46; exactitude 30; 10 lines, 6 circles.

*b)* One can operate a little more simply.

I draw :math:`OO'`, :math:`O'O''`, :math:`OO''` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (6R_1 + 3R_2)`.

Through :math:`O''` I draw a parallel to :math:`OO'` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 4C_1 + 2C_3)`; in :math:`O'` and :math:`O''` and in :math:`O` and :math:`O''` I have parallel diameters.

I thus have the four centers of similitude by :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (8R_1 + 4R_2)`, and the four axes then by :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (8R_1 + 4R_2)`,

In all: :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (24R_1 + 12R_2 + 4C_1 + 2C_3)`; simplicity 42; exactitude 28; 12 lines, 4 circles.

**XLVI.** — *Two points* :math:`A` *and* :math:`B` *being located on a straight line, locate the harmonic conjugate* :math:`C'` *of a given point* :math:`C`, *with respect to* :math:`A` *and* :math:`B`.

I draw an arbitrary straight line :math:`CDE` passing through :math:`C`, then two arbitrary straight lines passing through :math:`A`: one cutting :math:`CD` at :math:`D`, the other cutting the same line at :math:`E` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (3R_1 + 3R_2)`.

I draw :math:`DB`, :math:`EB` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (4R_1 + 2R_2)`.

:math:`DB` cuts :math:`AE` at :math:`F`, :math:`EB` cuts :math:`AD` at :math:`G`; I draw :math:`FG` cutting :math:`AB` at :math:`C` [i.e., :math:`C'`] :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2)`.

In all: :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (9R_1 + 6R_2)`; simplicity 15; exactitude 9; 6 lines.

We have just given, in what precedes, the principal constructions, that is to say those most often encountered in executing a solution; thus, with their symbols all calculated here, it will be easy and brief to find the total symbol of any construction; 
let us note that these fundamental symbols must never be used without examination; they can, *most often*, be simplified by the particular circumstances of the drawing one is executing, on account of lines already traced, etc.
