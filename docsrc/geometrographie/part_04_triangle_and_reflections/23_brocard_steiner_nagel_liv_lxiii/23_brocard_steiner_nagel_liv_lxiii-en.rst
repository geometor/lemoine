Problems LIV–LXIII: Brocard, Steiner, Tarry, Gergonne, Spieker, Nagel & Neuberg
===============================================================================

.. rubric:: Émile Lemoine — *La Géométrographie* (English Translation, pp. 45–52, fols. ``0061–0068``)

.. folio:: 0061
   :page: p. 45

Across pages 45–52, :person:`Lemoine <lemoine>` completes his tour of nineteenth-century triangle geometry (:cite:`altshiller-court-1952`, :cite:`kimberling-etc`), achieving dramatic operational reductions on every major remarkable point:

**Problem LIV — Single :term:`Brocard Point <points-de-brocard>` :math:`\omega` (:math:`\angle\omega AC = \angle\omega CB = \angle\omega BA`).**
Using :person:`Henri Brocard <brocard>`'s 1881 Algiers angle construction on three equal-radius vertex circles :math:`A(R), B(R), C(R)`:

.. math::

   \mathrm{Op.} : \left(8R_1 + 4R_2 + 10C_1 + 6C_3\right), \qquad S = 28, \quad E = 18 \quad (4\text{ lines}, 6\text{ circles}).

.. folio:: 0062
   :page: p. 46

**Problem LV — Both Brocard Points :math:`\omega` and :math:`\omega'` Simultaneously (:math:`S = 38` vs. Brocard's :math:`S = 74`).**
Reusing the exact same circles :math:`A(R), B(R), C(R)` and the Brocard-angle chord still held in the compass adds only :math:`4R_1 + 2R_2 + 2C_1 + 2C_3` to locate :math:`\omega'` as well (:ref:`concordance-brocard-points`):

.. math::

   \mathrm{Op.} : \left(12R_1 + 6R_2 + 12C_1 + 8C_3\right), \qquad S = 38, \quad E = 24 \quad (6\text{ lines}, 8\text{ circles}),

nearly halving :person:`Brocard <brocard>`'s own letter construction (:math:`S = 74`, dissected on pp. 58–59)!

**Problem LVI — :person:`Steiner <steiner>` Point :math:`X(99)` (via :person:`John Casey <casey>`'s *Sequel to Euclid*, 5th ed.).**
Sweeping :math:`A(BC), B(CA), C(AB)` (:math:`9C_1 + 3C_3`) meeting pairwise on the circumcircle at :math:`A_1, B_1, C_1`, intersecting :math:`B_1 C_1` with :math:`BC` at :math:`A_2` and :math:`C_1 A_1` with :math:`AC` at :math:`B_2`, and joining :math:`AA_2` with :math:`BB_2` (:math:`8R_1 + 4R_2`): :math:`8R_1 + 4R_2 + 9C_1 + 3C_3` (:math:`S = 24, E = 17`, 4 lines, 3 circles).

**Problem LVII — :person:`Gaston Tarry <tarry>` Point :math:`X(98)` (Antipodal to Steiner Point on Circumcircle).**
Tracing the circumcircle (:math:`4R_1 + 2R_2 + 5C_1 + 4C_3`), locating Steiner point :math:`R` via :math:`A(BC)` and :math:`B_1 C_1, AA_2`, and drawing diameter :math:`RO`: :math:`10R_1 + 5R_2 + 8C_1 + 5C_3` (:math:`S = 28, E = 18`, 5 lines, 5 circles).

.. folio:: 0063
   :page: p. 47

**Problem LVIII — Metric Lengths :math:`\frac{S}{R}` and :math:`\frac{2S}{R}` (Triangle Area / Circumradius).**
Noting that the projections :math:`A'_b, A'_c` of altitude foot :math:`A'` onto :math:`AC, AB` satisfy :math:`A'_b A'_c = S/R`, reflection via XVIII *bis* yields :math:`S/R` in :math:`6R_1 + 3R_2 + 12C_1 + 6C_3` (:math:`S = 27, E = 18`), while doubling via the reflections :math:`A'_{1c} A'_{1b} = 2S/R` needs only **1 line and 5 circles**: :math:`2R_1 + R_2 + 10C_1 + 5C_3` (:math:`S = 18, E = 12`)!

.. folio:: 0064
   :page: p. 48

**Problem LIX — :person:`Gergonne <gergonne>` Point :math:`X(7)` (and All Four Gergonne Points).**
A single Gergonne point costs :math:`10R_1 + 5R_2 + 9C_1 + 3C_3` (:math:`S = 27, E = 19`, 5 lines, 3 circles); all four Gergonne points together cost :math:`22R_1 + 11R_2 + 9C_1 + 3C_3` (:math:`S = 45, E = 31`, 11 lines, 3 circles).

**Problem LX — Perimeter Centroid (:person:`Spieker <spieker>` Center :math:`X(10)`).**
Sweeping :math:`B(a), A(a)` and three further circles of radius :math:`a` yields the Spieker center in :math:`8R_1 + 4R_2 + 10C_1 + 7C_3` (:math:`S = 29, E = 18`, 4 lines, 7 circles).

.. folio:: 0065
   :page: p. 49

**Problem LXI — :person:`Nagel <nagel>` Point :math:`X(8)` (Trilinears :math:`\frac{p - a}{a} : \frac{p - b}{b} : \frac{p - c}{c}`).**
Assuming :math:`a > b > c`, sweep :math:`A(a)` cutting :math:`AC` at :math:`\beta` and :math:`AB` at :math:`\gamma` (:math:`3C_1 + C_3`), sweep :math:`C(C\beta)` cutting :math:`CB` at :math:`\beta_1` and :math:`B(B\gamma)` cutting :math:`BC` at :math:`\gamma_1` (:math:`4C_1 + 2C_3`), and draw :math:`\beta\beta_1, \gamma\gamma_1` (:math:`4R_1 + 2R_2`)—meeting directly at :math:`X(8)` **without constructing a single excircle** (:ref:`concordance-nagel-point-x8`):

.. math::

   \mathrm{Op.} : \left(4R_1 + 2R_2 + 7C_1 + 3C_3\right), \qquad S = 16, \quad E = 11 \quad (2\text{ lines}, 3\text{ circles vs. classical }S = 34).

**Problem LXII — :person:`Neuberg <neuberg>` Radical Center :math:`\Phi` (:math:`\frac{a^2b^2 + a^2c^2 - b^2c^2}{a}`, etc.).**

.. folio:: 0066
   :page: p. 50

Constructing the two Brocardians of the Lemoine line via a rhombus parallel through :math:`B` and two symmedian feet gives :math:`\Phi` in :math:`10R_1 + 5R_2 + 15C_1 + 7C_3` (:math:`S = 37, E = 25`, 5 lines, 7 circles).

**Problem LXIII — Point :math:`W` (:math:`\frac{a^4 - b^2 c^2}{a}`, etc., :math:`S = 148 \to S = 62`).**

.. folio:: 0067
   :page: p. 51

Evaluating :math:`W` directly via five scaled ratios from Problem XXXIV *bis* and trilinear plotting (XLIX) costs a staggering :math:`32R_1 + 16R_2 + 68C_1 + 32C_3` (:math:`S = 148, E = 100`, 16 lines, 32 circles!).

.. folio:: 0068
   :page: p. 52

On page 52, Lemoine slashes that figure by more than half—down to **:math:`S = 62`**—using a synthetic concurrency theorem for :math:`W`, bringing the 63-problem constructive canon to a triumphant close before turning to *Observations diverses*.
