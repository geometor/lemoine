Problems L–LIII: Centroid X(2), Lemoine Point X(6) & Lemoine Line
=================================================================

.. rubric:: Émile Lemoine — *La Géométrographie* (English Translation, pp. 42–45, fols. ``0058–0061``)

.. folio:: 0058
   :page: p. 42

**L.** — *Place the center of gravity of a triangle* :g:`ABC`.

The coordinates of the center of gravity being :math:`\frac{1}{a}, \frac{1}{b}, \frac{1}{c}`, in order to find, without regard to the properties of this point, *lengths* proportional to these quantities, the simplest way would be, I think, to multiply them by :math:`a^2`, which would give:

.. math::

   a, \frac{a^2}{b}, \frac{a^2}{c},

to construct:

.. math::

   b' = \frac{a^2}{b}, \qquad c' = \frac{a^2}{c},

which would give (construction XXXIV) a simplicity 30, and to construct the corresponding point by construction XLIX. In all, a simplicity 84.

Here are other means, the only practical ones, deduced from the properties of the triangle:

.. folio:: 0059
   :page: p. 43

*a)* One uses the following property:
If :g:`A'` and :g:`B'` are the midpoints of :g:`BC` and :g:`CA`, :g:`AA'` and :g:`BB'` intersect at the center of gravity.

.. math::

   \mathrm{Op.} : \left(8R_1 + 4R_2 + 3C_1 + 3C_3\right); \quad \text{simplicity } 18; \quad \text{exactitude } 11; \quad 4\text{ lines}, 3\text{ circles}.

*b)* One uses the following property:

If one constructs a parallelogram :g:`CABA''`, :g:`AA'` passes through the center of gravity.

I describe the circles :g:`B(CA)` and :g:`A(CB)` which intersect at :g:`C'` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (6C_1 + 2C_3)`.

I trace :g:`C'B` which cuts :g:`B(CA)` at :g:`A'` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2)`.

I trace :g:`CC'`, :g:`AA'` which intersect at the sought point :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (4R_1 + 2R_2)`.

.. math::

   \mathrm{Op.} : \left(6R_1 + 3R_2 + 6C_1 + 2C_3\right); \quad \text{simplicity } 17; \quad \text{exactitude } 12; \quad 3\text{ lines}, 2\text{ circles}.

This is the simplest construction we know for obtaining the center of gravity.

**LI.** — *Place the* :term:`LEMOINE point <point-de-lemoine>` :g:`K` *of a triangle* :g:`ABC`.

Its normal coordinates being immediately given by the sides of the triangle, it would be placed by construction XLIX; but it is constructed in a very large number of simpler ways; we have studied six (which could, moreover, be better executed than we did then). (See *J. E.*, 1889, p. 34.)

I give here only the simplest:

On :g:`AC` I take :math:`AC' = AB` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (2C_1 + C_3)`; then leaving the point at :g:`A`, I take on :g:`AB`, :math:`AB' = AC`, :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (C_1 + C_3)`.

I describe the circumference :g:`C'(AC)` whose radius is between the legs of the compass :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (C_1 + C_3)`.

I take again the length :g:`AB` and describe :g:`B'(AB)` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (3C_1 + C_3)`. :g:`C'(AC)` and :g:`B'(AB)` intersect at :g:`A'`.

I draw the symmedian :g:`AA'` which contains the point :g:`K` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2)`.

I draw the other symmedian :g:`BB'` making a saving of :math:`C_1` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 2C_1 + 3C_3)`.

In all:

.. math::

   \mathrm{op.} : \left(4R_1 + 2R_2 + 13C_1 + 8C_3\right); \quad \text{simplicity } 27; \quad \text{exactitude } 17; \quad 2\text{ lines}, 8\text{ circles}.

If I had two compasses, I could save :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (4C_1 + C_3)` and I would have for symbol:

.. math::

   \mathrm{Op.} : \left(4R_1 + 2R_2 + 9C_1 + 7R_3\right); \quad \text{simplicity } 22; \quad \text{exactitude } 13; \quad 2\text{ lines}, 7\text{ circles}.

One sees from the foregoing that *one can draw a symmedian by the symbol:*

.. math::

   \mathrm{Op.} : \left(2R_1 + R_2 + 7C_1 + 4C_3\right); \quad \text{simplicity } 14; \quad \text{exactitude } 9; \quad 1\text{ line}, 4\text{ circles}.

   :caption: Problem LI: Lemoine's Symmedian Point K = X(6) constructed in only 2 traced lines and 8 circles (4R₁ + 2R₂ + 13C₁ + 8C₃, S = 27 vs. trilinear S = 54).

**LII.** — *Draw the* :term:`LEMOINE line <droite-de-lemoine>`.

I make at :g:`A` and at :g:`B`, on the opposite side of :g:`AB` from point :g:`C`, the angles :math:`BAA'`, :math:`ABB'` equal to :g:`C`.
:g:`A'` being on :g:`CB`, :g:`B'` on :g:`CA`, I trace :g:`A'B'`; it is the sought straight line.

.. math::

   \mathrm{Op.} : \left(6R_1 + 3R_2 + 6C_1 + 4C_3\right); \quad \text{simplicity } 19; \quad \text{exactitude } 12; \quad 3\text{ lines}; \quad 4\text{ circles}.

A very interesting case, but also much more delicate than the problem of finding a given construction as simply as possible, will arise very often in the geometry of the triangle:
that is, to combine a construction which gives the simplest means of finding, within one same set of constructions, several results which one equally needs. If the reader wishes to practice on a few simple cases, he will quickly see, if he still doubts it, that there exists a genuinely new *art* of geometric constructions.
This research requires that one possess thoroughly the geometry of the triangle, that one have great presence of mind in choosing the constructions when making the sketch of one's construction, and much reflection.

It will often happen that combining the simplest constructions for each isolated result will not at all yield
the simplest sought result, which will be obtained by different paths. The problem quickly becomes complicated and often presents difficulties that the so simple theory of the art of constructions could not have led one to foresee.

We shall briefly give a most elementary example of this.

**LIII.** — *Place the center of gravity and the point of* LEMOINE *of a triangle* :g:`ABC` *in one and the same construction.*

Adding constructions LI and LII would give us a symbol of simplicity 43, which would evidently simplify somewhat by using in the constructions the circles of the same radius
:math:`a, b` or :math:`c`, which one can trace for one of the constructions and which would serve for the other when their center happens to be placed on the drawing at the moment one has the suitable length between the legs of the compass, but it is easy to foresee that the total count
would exceed 32, for at the moment we would place :g:`K` we would have at least 26 (since that is the number we consider as given by the simplest construction of :g:`K`), and it would remain to find the barycenter, which would require at least drawing two straight lines or six elementary operations,
since no median has been traced in the construction of :g:`K`. The preceding lines are not a *rigorous* demonstration that 32 would be exceeded, but they show it sufficiently.

We shall give a construction which places both points by the symbol:

.. folio:: 0061
   :page: p. 45

.. math::

   \mathrm{Op.} : \left(12R_1 + 6R_2 + 9C_1 + 5C_3\right); \quad \text{simplicity } 32; \quad \text{exactitude } 21; \quad 6\text{ lines}, 5\text{ circles}.

I trace the three circles :g:`A(R)`, :g:`B(R)`, :g:`C(R)` of the same radius :math:`R` sufficient for them to intersect two by two :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (3C_1 + 3C_3)`.

By means of two of their intersections, I place the midpoints :g:`A'` and :g:`B'` of :g:`BC` and :g:`CA` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (4R_1 + 2R_2)`.

I trace :g:`AA'`, :g:`BB'`, which places the barycenter :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (4R_1 + 2R_2)`.

I take on the arcs of :g:`A(R)` and :g:`B(R)` comprised between the sides of angles :g:`A` and :g:`B` of the triangle the arcs which place the points where the arcs are cut by the symmedians of :g:`A` and :g:`B`
(we know that the medians and the symmedians of the same angle, symmetric with respect to the bisector of that angle, make respectively equal angles with the sides of the angle) :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (6C_1 + 2C_3)`;

And finally I trace the symmedians from :g:`A` and :g:`B`, which places point :g:`K` :math:`\mathrm{op.} : (4R_1 + 2R_2)`.

Readers may exercise themselves on the simplest construction to obtain in one and the same set:

* The point of concurrency of the altitudes, the center of gravity, the point of *Lemoine*;
* The center of the inscribed circle and that of the circumscribed circle;
* The point of *Nagel* and the point of *Gergonne*, etc., etc.
