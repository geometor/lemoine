Émile Lemoine (1840–1912)
=========================

**Émile Michel Hyacinthe Lemoine** (22 November 1840, Quimper – 21 February 1912, Paris) was one of the central architects of late nineteenth-century synthetic geometry. Educated at the *Prytanée National Militaire* of La Flèche and the *École Polytechnique* (entering in 1860), Lemoine combined a career as a civil and municipal gas engineer in Paris with a lifelong devotion to mathematics, chamber music—founding the legendary musical society *La Trompette*, for which Camille Saint-Saëns composed his *Septet*, Op. 65—and collaborative scientific correspondence.

Two Mathematical Revolutions
----------------------------

1. **Modern Geometry of the Triangle (Lyon, 1873)**:
   At the 1873 Lyon Congress of the *Association Française pour l'Avancement des Sciences* (AFAS), Lemoine presented *Sur quelques propriétés d'un point remarquable du triangle*, discovering the concurrency of the three symmedians at the **Lemoine point** :math:`K = X(6)` (minimizing the sum of squared distances to the sides of a triangle) along with the first and second Lemoine circles. Alongside Henri Brocard and Joseph Neuberg, Lemoine launched the international renaissance of triangle geometry catalogued today in ``geometor/etc`` (:external+etc:doc:`index`).

2. **Géométrographie (Oran 1888, Pau 1892, Scientia 1902)**:
   Asking how one can objectively compare two valid constructions for the same geometric locus, Lemoine decomposed ruler-and-compass drafting into five irreducible operations (:math:`R_1, R_2, C_1, C_2, C_3`), establishing quantitative measures of simplicity (:math:`S`) and exactitude (:math:`E`). In 1894, together with Charles-Ange Laisant, he founded *L'Intermédiaire des Mathématiciens*, and in 1895 the French Academy of Sciences awarded him the *Prix Francœur* repeatedly until his death.
