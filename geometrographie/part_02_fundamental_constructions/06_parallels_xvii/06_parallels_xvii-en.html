Problem XVII: Parallel Through an External Point¶

Émile Lemoine — La Géométrographie (English Translation, pp. 9–10, fols. 0025–0026)

XVII. — Through a point A taken outside a straight line BC, to draw a parallel to that straight line.

The classical method gives:

\(\mathrm{Op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 5C_1 + 3C_3)\); simplicity 11; exactitude 7; 1 straight line, 3 circles [*] .

But here are two which give simpler results and which were pointed out to me by M. Tarry (Gaston):

1° Through A I pass a circle cutting BC at B and at C \(\mathrm{op.} : (C_1 + C_3)\).

I take BA and I trace the circle CBA which cuts the first circle at D and I join AD \(\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 3C_1 + C_3)\).

Symbol: \(\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 4C_1 + 2C_3)\); simplicity 9; exactitude 6; 1 straight line, 2 circles.

2° I construct a rhombus ABCD:

\(\mathrm{Op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 3C_1 + 3C_3)\); simplicity 9; exactitude 5; 1 straight line, 3 circles.

f. 0026 · p. 10

REMARK. — These simplifications are important on account of the frequency of this construction in drafting plates.

3° Case where the straight line BC, not drawn, is given by two points B and C.

I take BC, I trace ABC \(\mathrm{op.} : (3C_1 + C_3)\).

I take AB, I trace CAB \(\mathrm{op.} : (3C_1 + C_3)\).

These two circles cut at D, I trace AD \(\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2)\).

Symbol: \(\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 6C_1 + 2C_3)\); simplicity 11; exactitude 8; 1 straight line, 2 circles.

[*]

I take this opportunity to make a remark not directly relating to our subject. It is known that the construction is performed thus: one describes a circle CCB, a circle BCB, a circle BAC which cuts CCB in two points D and D; CD is parallel to AB.

I sought the locus of D when the radius varies. One immediately finds that it has for equation: \(\rho = \frac{l}{\sin\frac{\omega}{2}}\). C being the pole, CD the polar axis, \(l\) the distance from C to AB.
