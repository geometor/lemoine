Problems XI–XVI: Copying Angles & Constructing Triangles¶

Émile Lemoine — La Géométrographie (English Translation, pp. 7–9, fols. 0023–0025)

In Book I of Euclid’s Elements (euclid-heath; see and ), Proposition I.23 transfers a rectilinear angle by joining two arbitrary points across its arms and invoking Proposition I.22 (triangle from three segments)—drawing an unnecessary auxiliary chord (\(4R_1 + 2R_2 + 8C_1 + C_2 + 4C_3\), \(S = 19, E = 13\)). Lemoine replaces this speculative detour with direct equal-radius arc transfer and proceeds systematically through the five classical triangle constructions:

Problem XI (Euclid I.23 — Equal Angle :math:`angle DAE` at Given Point :math:`B` on Traced Line :math:`BC`). Sweep \(A(AE)\) of arbitrary radius cutting \(AD\) at \(D\) and \(AC\) at \(E\) (\(C_1 + C_3\)), then with the same unaltered radius sweep \(B(AE)\) cutting \(BC\) at \(F\) (\(C_1 + C_3\)). Capture chord \(DE\) and sweep \(F(DE)\) cutting \(B(AE)\) at \(H\) (\(3C_1 + C_3\)), and draw \(BH\) (\(2R_1 + R_2\)):

\[\mathrm{Op.} : \left(2R_1 + R_2 + 5C_1 + 3C_3\right), \qquad S = 11, \quad E = 7 \quad (1\text{ line}, 3\text{ circles}).\]

Problem XII — Third Angle :math:`gamma` of a Triangle Given Angles :math:`alpha` and :math:`beta`. Trace an arbitrary straight line \(AB\) (\(R_2\)). With one shared arbitrary radius \(R\), sweep three circles \(\alpha(R)\), \(\beta(R)\), and \(O(R)\) centered at an indeterminate point \(O\) on \(AB\) cutting \(AB\) at \(B\) (\(2C_1 + C_2 + 3C_3\)). Step the chord of \(\alpha\) from \(B\) to \(E\) on \(O(R)\) (\(3C_1 + C_3\)), then step the chord of \(\beta\) from \(E\) to \(H\) (\(3C_1 + C_3\)), and draw \(OH\) (\(2R_1 + R_2\)). The supplementary angle \(\angle HOA\) equals \(\gamma\) without ever drawing the intermediate ray \(OE\) (saving \(2R_1 + R_2 + 2C_1\) over Lemoine’s 1888 Oran evaluation):

\[\mathrm{Op.} : \left(2R_1 + 2R_2 + 8C_1 + C_2 + 5C_3\right) \quad [\text{printed } 6C_3\text{ on p. 7}], \qquad S = 18, \quad E = 10.\]
f. 0024 · p. p. 8

Problem XIII — Triangle Given Side :math:`a` and Adjacent Angles :math:`angle xoy, angle x’o’y’`. Trace an arbitrary line \(BC\) and lay off \(BC = a\) from indeterminate \(B\) (\(R_2 + 2C_1 + C_2 + C_3\)), retaining radius \(BC\) to sweep \(o(BC)\), \(o'(BC)\), and \(C(BC)\) (\(3C_1 + 3C_3\)). Transfer chords \(xy\) to \(C'\) on \(B(BC)\) and \(x'y'\) to \(B'\) on \(C(BC)\) (\(6C_1 + 2C_3\)), and draw \(CB', BC'\) meeting at \(A\) (\(4R_1 + 2R_2\)): \(4R_1 + 3R_2 + 11C_1 + C_2 + 6C_3\) (\(S = 25, E = 16\), 3 lines, 6 circles). Operating directly on the given side reduces this to \(4R_1 + 2R_2 + 10C_1 + 6C_3\) (\(S = 22\)), or on one given angle to \(2R_1 + R_2 + 8C_1 + 4C_3\) (\(S = 15\)).

Problem XIV — Triangle Given Two Sides :math:`b, c` and Included Angle :math:`angle xoy`.

f. 0025 · p. p. 9

Draw an arbitrary line (\(R_2\)), lay off \(AC = b\) from indeterminate \(A\) (\(2C_1 + C_2 + C_3\)), sweep \(o(AC)\) (\(C_1 + C_3\)), transfer chord \(xy\) to \(B'\) on \(A(AC)\) (\(3C_1 + C_3\)), draw \(AB'\) (\(2R_1 + R_2\)), lay off \(AB = c\) on \(AB'\) (\(3C_1 + C_3\)), and join \(CB\) (\(2R_1 + R_2\)): \(4R_1 + 3R_2 + 9C_1 + C_2 + 4C_3\) (\(S = 21, E = 14\), 3 lines, 4 circles).

Problem XV — Triangle Given Two Sides :math:`a, b` and Opposite Angle :math:`B`. When both ambiguous-case solutions exist: \(6R_1 + 4R_2 + 9C_1 + C_2 + 4C_3\) (\(S = 24, E = 16\), 4 lines, 4 circles).

Problem XVI (Euclid I.22 — Triangle from Three Given Sides :math:`a, b, c`). Drawing the initial base carrier as an arbitrary line (\(R_2\)) rather than joining two pre-placed points saves three operations over textbook execution (\(S = 22\), XVI — Triangle from Three Given Sides):

\[\mathrm{Op.} : \left(4R_1 + 3R_2 + 8C_1 + C_2 + 3C_3\right), \qquad S = 19, \quad E = 13 \quad (3\text{ lines}, 3\text{ circles}).\]
