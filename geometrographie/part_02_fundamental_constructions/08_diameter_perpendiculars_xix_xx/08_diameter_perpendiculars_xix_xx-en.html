Problems XIX–XX: Circle Center & Perpendiculars¶

Émile Lemoine — La Géométrographie (English Translation, pp. 10–12, fols. 0026–0028)

XIX. — To describe a circle upon a given straight line AB as diameter.

One takes the midpoint O of AB \(\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 2C_1 + 2C_3)\).

One takes the length OA, then describes OOA \(\mathrm{op.} : (2C_1 + C_3)\).

Symbol: \(\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 4C_1 + 3C_3)\); simplicity 10; exactitude 6; 1 straight line, 3 circles.

XX. — To trace through a point C a perpendicular to a straight line AB.

1° The point C is outside the straight line.

Classical method.

a) Symbol: \(\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 3C_1 + 3C_3)\); simplicity 9; exactitude 5; 1 straight line, 3 circles.

f. 0027 · p. 11

Another method.

b) B being any point of AB, I describe BBC which cuts AB at A \(\mathrm{op.} : (C_2 + C_1 + C_3)\).

I take AC and describe AAC which cuts BBC at C; I trace CC \(\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 2C_1 + C_3)\).

Total symbol: \(\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 3C_1 + C_2 + 2C_3)\); simplicity 9; exactitude 6; 1 straight line, 2 circles.

2° The point C is on AB.

Classical method.

a) Same symbol and same elementary operations as when C is outside the line; the following method is slightly simpler.

b) I place one leg at an arbitrary point O outside AB; I place the other leg at C and describe the circumference OOC which also cuts AB at A; I trace AO which cuts OOC at C \(\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + C_1 + C_3)\).

I trace CC \(\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2)\).

Symbol: \(\mathrm{op.} : (4R_1 + 2R_2 + C_1 + C_3)\); simplicity 8; exactitude 5; 2 straight lines, 1 circle.

REMARK. — This method b which is classically given for the case where the straight line AB cannot be produced beyond A is simpler than the general classical method a given when C is arbitrary on AB; b must therefore always be employed, and there is no need to separate the case where C falls at A,

A being the extremity of AB when that position is imposed by the dimensions of the drawing.

If one wishes to erect an arbitrary perpendicular to AB, one then has:

Symbol: \(\mathrm{op.} : (4R_1 + 2R_2 + C_3)\); simplicity 7; exactitude 4; 2 straight lines, 1 circle.

One may also, through A and B, arbitrary points of AB, trace two arbitrary circles; they intersect along a perpendicular to AB.

\(\mathrm{Op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 2C_2 + 2C_3)\); simplicity 7; exactitude 4; 1 straight line, 2 circles.

If one wishes to erect an arbitrary perpendicular to a straight line (not drawn) given by two points A and B, one describes AR, BR; R and R being arbitrary, the intersection of these two circles solves the question.

\(\mathrm{Op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 2C_1 + 2C_3)\); simplicity 7; exactitude 4; 1 straight line, 2 circles.

To drop from a point C external to a straight line (not drawn) given by two points A and B, a perpendicular upon its direction.

One draws AAC, BBC; the intersection of these two circles is the required perpendicular.

f. 0028 · p. 12

\(\mathrm{Op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 4C_1 + 2C_3)\); simplicity 9; exactitude 6; 1 straight line, 2 circles.

REMARK I. — If one wishes to draw the perpendicular at A to a straight line AB not drawn and given by two points A and B, one must proceed thus:

Trace BR, AR with a same arbitrary radius R intersecting at C, then CR passing through A and B, trace BC cutting CR at C, and trace AC.

\(\mathrm{Op.} : (4R_1 + 2R_2 + 3C_1 + 3C_3)\); simplicity 12; exactitude 7; 2 straight lines, 3 circles.

It is rather curious to remark that when the straight line is given by two points A and B, it is simpler to draw a perpendicular to it through an arbitrary point than through one of the given points.

REMARK II. — When, in a construction, one has to erect perpendiculars at \(n\) given points ABCD

of given straight lines LMNP, there is an advantage, if \(n > 5\), in operating thus:

I draw a first perpendicular at A to M by an operation whose simplicity is 8 (see XX, 2° b).

I describe from all the given points as centers circumferences of the same radius; simplicity: \(2n\).

I take upon the circumference traced at A the chord of the quadrant; simplicity: 2.

I transfer it to all the other circumferences and, by their means, I trace the perpendiculars; simplicity: \(5(n - 1)\).

Therefore they will be traced by an operation of simplicity \(5 + 7n\), instead of \(8n\) which the general construction would give. There will therefore be an advantage in taking it if:

\[5 + 7n < 8n \quad \text{or} \quad 5 < n.\]
