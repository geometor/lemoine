Problems XVIII & XVIII bis: Perpendicular Bisector & Point Reflection¶

Émile Lemoine — La Géométrographie (English Translation, p. 10, fols. 0026)

Problem XVIII (Euclid I.10 — Perpendicular Bisector & Midpoint of :g:`AB`). To trace the perpendicular at the midpoint of the segment joining two given points A and B (or to locate the midpoint M of a traced segment AB).

Whereas Euclid sets the compass specifically to radius \(AB\) (\(2C_1 + C_3\) at A then \(C_1 + C_3\) at B, costing \(2R_1 + R_2 + 3C_1 + 2C_3\), \(S = 8, E = 5\)), Lemoine notes that any arbitrary shared opening \(R > AB/2\) suffices: sweep AR (\(C_1 + C_3\)) and BR (\(C_1 + C_3\)) intersecting at P and Q, and draw PQ (\(2R_1 + R_2\)) bisecting AB into AM and MB:

\[\mathrm{Op.} : \left(2R_1 + R_2 + 2C_1 + 2C_3\right), \qquad S = 7, \quad E = 4 \quad (1\text{ line}, 2\text{ circles}).\]

Problem XVIII bis — Symmetric Reflection :math:`A’` of a Point :math:`A` Across a Traced Line :math:`BC` (Zero Straight Lines!). Classical textbooks drop a perpendicular from \(A\) to \(BC\), extend it, and transfer the foot distance (\(S = 15\), 2 lines, 3 circles). Lemoine reflects \(A\) by compass alone without touching the straightedge: place one compass leg at an indeterminate point on \(BC\) (\(C_2\)) and the other on \(A\) (\(C_1\)), sweep an arc (\(C_3\)), and repeat from a second indeterminate point on \(BC\) (\(C_1 + C_2 + C_3\)). The second intersection of the two circles is the exact mirror image \(A'\):

\[\mathrm{Op.} : \left(2C_1 + 2C_2 + 2C_3\right), \qquad S = 6, \quad E = 4 \quad (0\text{ lines}, 2\text{ circles}).\]

When two determined points \(B, C\) already lie on the mirror line, sweeping \(B(BA)\) and \(C(CA)\) costs \(4C_1 + 2C_3\) (\(S = 6, E = 4\))—the exact compass-only reflection of Mascheroni (mascheroni-1797, Libro IV, § 78)!
