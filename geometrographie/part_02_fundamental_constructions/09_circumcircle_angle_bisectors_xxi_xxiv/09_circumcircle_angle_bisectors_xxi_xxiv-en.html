Problems XXI–XXIV: Circumcircle & Angle Bisectors¶

Émile Lemoine — La Géométrographie (English Translation, pp. 12–14, fols. 0028–0030)

Problem XXI (Euclid IV.5 — Circumcircle & Circumcenter :math:`X(3)` Through Three Points :math:`A, B, C`). Open the compass to a single arbitrary radius \(R\) greater than half of both \(AB\) and \(BC\). Sweep \(B(R)\) once (\(C_1 + C_3\)), then \(A(R)\) and \(C(R)\) (\(2C_1 + 2C_3\)). Sharing \(B(R)\) across both perpendicular bisectors cuts the auxiliary circle count from four to three; tracing the two mediatrices (\(4R_1 + 2R_2\)) fixes circumcenter \(O = X(3)\) (kimberling-etc, ), and sweeping \(O(A)\) (\(2C_1 + C_3\)) completes the circumcircle (saving 2 operations over textbook \(S = 17\), XXI — Circumcircle and Circumcenter X(3) Through Three Given Points):

\[\mathrm{Op.} : \left(4R_1 + 2R_2 + 5C_1 + 4C_3\right), \qquad S = 15, \quad E = 9 \quad (2\text{ lines}, 4\text{ circles}).\]

Problem XXII (Euclid I.9 — Angle Bisector of :math:`angle BAC`). Keeping one single sufficient radius \(R\) for the initial arc centered at vertex \(A\) (\(C_1 + C_3\), cutting the arms at \(B\) and \(C\)) and for both intersecting arcs \(B(R)\) and \(C(R)\) (\(2C_1 + 2C_3\)), then joining \(A\) to their intersection (\(2R_1 + R_2\)), yields:

\[\mathrm{Op.} : \left(2R_1 + R_2 + 3C_1 + 3C_3\right), \qquad S = 9, \quad E = 5 \quad (1\text{ line}, 3\text{ circles}),\]

saving one \(C_1\) over Euclid I.9 (\(S = 10\), XXII — Bisecting a Given Rectilinear Angle).

f. 0029 · p. 13

If the angle is specified merely by three unjoined points \(B, A, C\), Lemoine traces \(AB\) (\(2R_1 + R_2\)), sweeps \(A(AC)\) cutting \(AB\) at \(C'\) (\(2C_1 + C_3\)), sweeps \(C(AC)\) and \(C'(AC)\) meeting at \(D\), and traces \(AD\) (\(2R_1 + R_2 + 2C_1 + 2C_3\)), totaling \(4R_1 + 2R_2 + 4C_1 + 3C_3\) (\(S = 13, E = 8\), 2 lines, 3 circles).

Problem XXIII (Euclid III.30 — Bisect a Given Circular Arc). Assuming the circle’s center is known (otherwise Problem XIX adds \(4R_1 + 2R_2 + 3C_2 + 3C_3\)), perpendicular bisection of the chord joining the arc’s endpoints takes \(2R_1 + R_2 + 2C_1 + 2C_3\) (\(S = 7, E = 4\), 1 line, 2 circles).

Problem XXIV — Bisector of the Angle Between Two Lines :math:`AB, CD` Whose Intersection :math:`X` Is Inaccessible. From an indeterminate point \(A\) on \(AB\), sweep \(A(R)\) cutting \(CD\) at \(C\) and \(AB\) at \(B\), and with the same radius sweep \(C(R)\) cutting \(CD\) at \(D\) (\(C_1 + C_2 + 2C_3\)). Sweep \(B(R)\) cutting \(C(R)\) at \(J\), and \(D(R)\) cutting \(A(R)\) at \(I\) (\(2C_1 + 2C_3\)); trace \(AJ\) and \(CI\) (\(4R_1 + 2R_2\)), meeting at a point \(M\) on the required bisector! Sweep \(M(R')\) cutting \(AB\) at \(H\) and \(CD\) at \(G\) (\(C_1 + C_3\)), find one equidistant point \(M'\) from \(G, H\), and join \(MM'\) (\(2R_1 + R_2 + 2C_1 + 2C_3\)):

\[\mathrm{Op.} : \left(6R_1 + 3R_2 + 6C_1 + C_2 + 7C_3\right), \qquad S = 23, \quad E = 13 \quad (3\text{ lines}, 7\text{ circles}).\]
f. 0030 · p. 14

Page 14 concludes the preliminary angle-and-arc block and opens the circle-tangency sequence (Problems XXV–XXVI).
