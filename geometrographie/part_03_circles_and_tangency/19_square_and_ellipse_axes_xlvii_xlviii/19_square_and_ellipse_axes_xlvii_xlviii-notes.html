Problems XLVII–XLVIII: Catalan’s Square & Axes of an Ellipse — Scholarly Notes¶

Archival Folio Provenance, Textual Collation & Cross-References (Probs. XLVII–XLVIII, fols. 0050–0052)

  • Primary Witness: Folios 0050–0052 (printed pp. 34–36); FIG. 9 appears on folio 0050 (p. 34).

  • Exact Algebra of FIG. 9: Setting \(A = (0, 0), B = (1, 0)\) gives \(K = (\tfrac{1}{2}, \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2})\), \(G = (\tfrac{3}{2}, \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2})\), \(I = (\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, \tfrac{1}{2})\), and \(KI^2 = (\tfrac{\sqrt{3}-1}{2})^2 + (\tfrac{1-\sqrt{3}}{2})^2 = 2 - \sqrt{3}\), which equals both \(KC^2 = (1 - \tfrac{1}{2})^2 + (1 - \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2})^2 = 2 - \sqrt{3}\) and \(KD^2\) identically!

  • Speculative vs. Constructive Contrast: Problem XLVIII is Lemoine’s signature case study proving that even masters like Michel Chasles routinely omit two-thirds of the physical drawing labor when a theorem yields full magnitudes instead of positioned semi-axes.
