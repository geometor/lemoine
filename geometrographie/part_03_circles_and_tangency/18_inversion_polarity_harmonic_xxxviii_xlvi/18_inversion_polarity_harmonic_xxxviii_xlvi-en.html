Problems XXXVIII–XLVI: Inversion, Polarity, Radical Axis & Harmonic Conjugates¶

Émile Lemoine — La Géométrographie (English Translation, pp. 29–33, fols. 0045–0049)

Problems XXXVIII–XLVI reveal the deepest structural discovery of Géométrographie: even when both instruments are allowed, pure compass-only (Mascheroni) or pure straightedge-only (Pappus, Steiner) syntheses frequently beat mixed constructions!

Problem XXXVIII (:term:`Circle Inversion <inversion>` — Inverse Point :math:`A’` of :math:`A` with Respect to :math:`O(R)`). 1. When :math:`OA > R/2` (Compass Alone!): Sweep \(A(AO)\) cutting \(O(R)\) at \(B, C\), then sweep \(B(R)\) and \(C(R)\) meeting at \(O\) and \(A'\) (XXXVIII — Inverse of a Given Point with Respect to a Given Circle):

\[\mathrm{Op.} : \left(5C_1 + 3C_3\right), \qquad S = 8, \quad E = 5 \quad (0\text{ lines}, 3\text{ circles}).\]

This exact 3-circle lemma powered August Adler’s 1890 conceptual proof of the Mohr–Mascheroni theorem (adler-1890)! 2. General case (:math:`OA < R/2`): Draw \(OA\), sweep \(A(R)\) cutting \(O(R)\) at \(B\), sweep \(B(R)\) cutting \(O(R)\) at \(D, D'\), and draw \(DD'\) cutting \(OA\) at \(A'\): \(4R_1 + 2R_2 + 4C_1 + 2C_3\) (\(S = 12, E = 8\), 2 lines, 2 circles). A 1-circle general method also exists in \(8R_1 + 4R_2 + C_1 + C_3\) (\(S = 14\)).

Problem XXXIX (:term:`Polar Line <polaire>` of :math:`A` with Respect to Circle :math:`O(R)` — Straightedge Alone!). Method (a): Draw two arbitrary secants through \(A\) cutting the circumference at \(B, B'\) and \(C, C'\) (\(2R_1 + 2R_2\)); intersect \(B'C', BC\) at \(D\) and \(BC', CB'\) at \(E\), then join \(ED\) (XXXIX — Polar Line of a Given Point with Respect to a Traced Circle):

\[\mathrm{Op.} : \left(12R_1 + 7R_2\right), \qquad S = 19, \quad E = 12 \quad (7\text{ lines}, 0\text{ circles}),\]

identical to Jakob Steiner § 10 (steiner-1833) and strictly beating classical ruler-and-compass (\(S = 25\))!

f. 0046 · p. 30

Method (b) drops a perpendicular from \(D\) to un-traced \(OA\) in \(8R_1 + 5R_2 + 4C_1 + 2C_3\) (also \(S = 19, E = 12\)). When \(OA > R/2\), non-general Method (c) yields \(4R_1 + 2R_2 + 5C_1 + 3C_3\) (\(S = 14, E = 9\), correcting Lemoine’s earlier 1888 Mathesis slip).

Problem XL — Pole :math:`L` of a Line :math:`XY` with Respect to :math:`O(R)`. 1° When \(XY\) cuts \(O(R)\) at \(M, N\): draw tangent at \(M\) (Problem XXV), sweep \(N(R)\) cutting \(M(R)\) at \(O'\), and draw \(OO'\) meeting the tangent at \(L\): \(4R_1 + 2R_2 + 5C_1 + 4C_3\) (\(S = 15, E = 9\), 2 lines, 4 circles).

f. 0047 · p. 31

2° When \(XY\) does not cut \(O(R)\) (or distance \(OF > R/2\)): drop perpendicular \(OF\) to \(XY\), sweep \(F(FO)\) cutting \(O(R)\) at \(H\), and sweep \(H(R)\) cutting \(OF\) at \(L\): \(2R_1 + R_2 + 7C_1 + 5C_3\) (\(S = 15, E = 9\), 1 line, 5 circles). Together, 1° and 2° achieve a uniform general cost of \(S = 15\)!

Problem XLI (:term:`Radical Axis <linie-der-gleichen-potenzen>` of Two Circles :math:`O(R), O’(R’)`). Sweep two completely arbitrary secant circles \(\omega(\rho), \omega'(\rho')\) (\(2C_3\)—zero alignment cost!) each intersecting both given circles, join pairwise common chords to locate two radical centers, and join them (XLI — Radical Axis of Two Non-Intersecting Traced Circles):

\[\mathrm{Op.} : \left(10R_1 + 5R_2 + 2C_3\right), \qquad S = 17, \quad E = 10 \quad (5\text{ lines}, 2\text{ circles}),\]

dramatically defeating Amédée Mannheim’s common-tangent construction (\(S = 58\), pp. 57–58)! (Tangent circles reduce to \(S = 10\), intersecting circles to \(2R_1 + R_2\), \(S = 3\).)

Problem XLII — Radical Center of Three Circles :math:`R_1, R_2, R_3`.

f. 0048 · p. 32

Across all five incidence cases (pp. 31–32), the symbol decreases from \(16R_1 + 8R_2 + 2C_3\) (\(S = 26\), disjoint) and \(16R_1 + 8R_2 + C_3\) (\(S = 25\), mutually tangent) down to \(8R_1 + 4R_2 + C_3\) (\(S = 13\)) and \(4R_1 + 2R_2\) (\(S = 6\), pairwise intersecting).

Problem XLIII — Plot Point :math:`M` from Cartesian Coordinates :math:`(x, y)` on Axes :math:`ox, oy` (Compass Alone!). Lay off \(OA = x\) on \(ox\), \(OB = y\) on \(oy\), and intersect \(A(y)\) with \(B(x)\) at \(M\): \(10C_1 + 4C_3\) (\(S = 14, E = 10\), 0 lines, 4 circles; or \(8C_1 + 4C_3\) with two compasses). In footnote (*) on p. 32, Lemoine thanks Gaston Tarry for two years of correspondence that uncovered numerous simplifications throughout this memoir.

Problems XLIV & XLV — Centers & Four Axes of Similitude of Two and Three Circles.

f. 0049 · p. 33

From Problem XXIX (FIG. 3), both centers of similitude \(V, V_1\) of two circles cost \(6R_1 + 3R_2 + 4C_1 + 2C_3\) (\(S = 15, E = 10\), 3 lines, 2 circles), while the four axes of similitude of three circles drop from \(S = 46\) to \(24R_1 + 12R_2 + 4C_1 + 2C_3\) (\(S = 42, E = 28\), 12 lines, 2 circles) via parallel diameters through \(O''\).

Problem XLVI (:term:`Harmonic Conjugate <conjugue-harmonique>` :math:`C’` of :math:`C` with Respect to :math:`A, B` — Straightedge Alone!). Draw arbitrary ray \(CDE\) through \(C\) and two rays \(AD, AE\) through \(A\) (\(3R_1 + 3R_2\)); draw \(DB, EB\) (\(4R_1 + 2R_2\)) cutting \(AE, AD\) at \(F, G\); draw \(FG\) (\(2R_1 + R_2\)) cutting \(AB\) at \(C'\) (Pappus Synagoge Book VII, pappus-synagoge; Steiner §§ 4–6; XLVI — Harmonic Conjugate of a Point with Respect to a Segment):

\[\mathrm{Op.} : \left(9R_1 + 6R_2\right), \qquad S = 15, \quad E = 9 \quad (6\text{ lines}, 0\text{ circles}).\]
