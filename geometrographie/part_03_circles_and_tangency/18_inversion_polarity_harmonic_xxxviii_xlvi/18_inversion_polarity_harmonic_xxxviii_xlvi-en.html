Problems XXXVIII–XLVI: Inversion, Polarity, Radical Axis & Harmonic Conjugates¶

Émile Lemoine — La Géométrographie (English Translation, pp. 29–33, fols. 0045–0049)

XXXVIII. — Locate the point \(A'\) reciprocal to a given point \(A\) with respect to a given circle of radius \(R\) and center \(O\).

Two cases to examine:

1° \(OA > \frac{R}{2}\).

I draw \(A(AO)\) cutting \(O(R)\) at \(B\) and \(C\). I draw \(B(R)\), \(C(R)\) intersecting at \(O\) and \(A\) [i.e., \(A'\)]. \(A'\) is the sought point.

\(\mathrm{Op.} : (5C_1 + 3C_3)\); simplicity 8; exactitude 5; 3 circles.

2° \(OA < \frac{R}{2}\).

I draw \(OA\); I draw \(A(R)\) cutting \(O(R)\) at \(B\) \(\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 3C_1 + C_3)\).

I draw \(B(R)\) cutting \(O(R)\) at \(D\) and \(D'\); I draw \(DD'\) cutting \(OA\) at \(A'\) \(\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + C_1 + C_3)\).

In all: \(\mathrm{op.} : (4R_1 + 2R_2 + 4C_1 + 2C_3)\); simplicity 12; exactitude 8; 2 circles, 2 lines.

Thus, in seeking the general symbol of a construction, it is this latter symbol that must be adopted to count the search for the reciprocal of a point \(A\) with respect to a circle of radius \(R\), if it does not follow from the general data that \(OA > \frac{R}{2}\).

Here is a construction that also applies whatever \(A\) may be, though it is somewhat more complicated.

I draw a circle with center \(A\) cutting the given circle at \(B\) and \(C\).

I draw \(AB\), \(AC\) cutting the given circle at \(B'\) and \(C'\) \(\mathrm{op.} : (4R_1 + 2R_2 + C_1 + C_3)\).

I draw \(B'C\), \(C'B\) intersecting at \(A'\) \(\mathrm{op.} : (4R_1 + 2R_2)\).

\(\mathrm{Op.} : (8R_1 + 4R_2 + C_1 + C_3)\); simplicity 14; exactitude 9; 4 lines, 1 circle.

XXXIX. — Draw the polar of a point \(A\) with respect to a circumference of center \(O\) and radius \(R\).

a) Through \(A\) I draw two arbitrary straight lines: the first cutting the circumference at \(B\) and \(B'\), the second at \(C\) and \(C'\) \(\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + 2R_2)\).

I draw \(B'C'\) and \(BC\) intersecting at \(D\), and \(BC'\), \(CB'\) intersecting at \(E\).

I draw \(ED\); this is the sought polar.

\(\mathrm{Op.} : (12R_1 + 7R_2)\); simplicity 19; exactitude 12; 7 lines.

f. 0046 · p. 30

b) I can also, \(D\) being marked as before, drop a perpendicular from \(D\) onto \(OA\) (without drawing \(OA\)) \(\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 4C_1 + 2C_3)\).

In all: \(\mathrm{op.} : (8R_1 + 5R_2 + 4C_1 + 2C_3)\); simplicity 19; exactitude 12; 5 lines, 2 circles.

I have not been able to find a general construction of the polar of a given point \(A\) with respect to a circle simpler than these two.

It is by error that I give 15 as Simplicity in my note in Mathesis, 1888, page 222. I had not noticed the case where \(OA < \frac{R}{2}\).

There are a great many particular constructions of the same problem.

c) Non-general construction applicable in the case where \(OA > \frac{R}{2}\).

I describe \(A(OA)\) cutting the given circle at \(B\) and \(C\).

I describe \(B(R)\), \(C(R)\) intersecting at \(A'\) reciprocal of \(A\) \(\mathrm{op.} : (5C_1 + 3C_3)\).

I draw \(OC\) cutting \(C(R)\) at \(D\); I draw \(DA'\) \(\mathrm{op.} : (4R_1 + 2R_2)\); this is the sought polar.

\(\mathrm{Op.} : (4R_1 + 2R_2 + 5C_1 + 3C_3)\); simplicity 14; exactitude 9; 2 lines, 3 circles.

If \(A\) is exterior to the given circle, one may also draw a circle on \(OA\) as diameter; the intersection of the two circles is the sought polar; the symbol is then:

\(\mathrm{Op.} : (6R_1 + 3R_2 + 4C_1 + 3C_3)\); simplicity 16; exactitude 10; 3 lines, 3 circles.

XL. — Locate the pole \(L\) of a straight line \(XY\) with respect to a given circumference of center \(O\) and radius \(R\).

Two cases to consider:

1° \(XY\) cuts circle \(O(R)\) at \(M\) and \(N\).

I draw the tangent at \(M\) to circle \(O(R)\) (see construction XXV) \(\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 4C_1 + 3C_3)\).

I draw circle \(N(R)\) cutting at \(O'\) the circle \(M(R)\) drawn to obtain the tangent at \(M\) \(\mathrm{op.} : (C_1 + C_3)\).

I draw \(OO'\) \(\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2)\); \(OO'\) cuts the tangent at \(M\) at the sought pole \(L\).

\(\mathrm{Op.} : (4R_1 + 2R_2 + 5C_1 + 4C_3)\); simplicity 15; exactitude 9; 2 lines, 4 circles.

2° \(XY\) does not cut circle \(O(R)\). (This solution applies even if \(XY\) cuts circle \(O(R)\), provided the distance from \(O\) to \(XY\) is greater than \(\frac{R}{2}\)).

f. 0047 · p. 31

From \(O\) I drop onto \(XY\) a perpendicular whose foot on \(XY\) is \(F\) and which cuts \(O(R)\) at \(K\) on the same side of \(O\) as \(F\) \(\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 3C_1 + 3C_3)\).

I describe \(F(FO)\) cutting \(O(R)\) at \(H\); I describe \(H(R)\) cutting \(OF\) at \(L\) \(\mathrm{op.} : (4C_1 + 2C_3)\).

\(L\) is the sought pole; for the two similar isosceles triangles \(OFH\), \(OHL\) have side \(OH\) in common, hence \(\overline{OH}^2\) or \(R^2 = OL \cdot OF\).

\(\mathrm{Op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 7C_1 + 5C_3)\); simplicity 15; exactitude 9; 1 line, 5 circles.

These two cases together form a general construction of simplicity 15, for if one is not applicable, the other is.

There are still a great many general constructions for the same problem, yet I know none as simple as the two I give here.

XLI. — Draw the radical axis of two given circumferences \(O(R)\), \(O'(R')\).

I draw two circumferences \(\omega(\rho)\), \(\omega'(\rho')\) each cutting both given circumferences, etc.

\(\mathrm{Op.} : (10R_1 + 5R_2 + 2C_3)\); simplicity 17; exactitude 10; 5 lines, 2 circles.

If the circumferences are tangent, the symbol reduces to that of the tangent at the point of contact \(\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 4C_1 + 3C_3)\).

If they intersect, to \(\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2)\).

XLII. — Locate the radical center of three circumferences \(R_1, R_2, R_3\).

a) \(R_1, R_2, R_3\) are exterior to one another, or else one of them, \(R_3\), for example, is tangent to only one of the first two.

One draws the two circumferences \(\omega(\rho)\), \(\omega'(\rho')\) of the preceding problem so that they cut all three given circumferences; one finds:

\(\mathrm{Op.} : (16R_1 + 8R_2 + 2C_3)\).

b) \(R_1\) and \(R_2\) are exterior and \(R_3\) touches \(R_1\) and \(R_2\), or they touch two by two.

I draw \(\omega(\rho)\) only; by means of \(\omega(\rho)\), I construct a point \(K_1\) of the radical axis of \(R_1\) and \(R_3\); I join \(K_1\) to the point of contact \(L_1\) of \(R_1\) and \(R_3\), \(L_1\) being located by drawing \(O_1 O_3\) \(\mathrm{op.} : (8R_1 + 4R_2 + C_3)\).

Likewise, I construct a point \(K_2\) of the radical axis of \(R_2\) and \(R_3\) and join \(K_2\) to the point of contact \(L_2\) of \(R_2\) and \(R_3\) \(\mathrm{op.} : (6R_1 + 3R_2)\).

In all: \(\mathrm{Op.} : (16R_1 + 8R_2 + C_3)\).

c) \(R_1, R_2\) intersect, \(R_3\) is exterior.

f. 0048 · p. 32

I draw \(\omega(\rho)\), \(\omega'(\rho')\); I determine the radical axis of \(R_1\) and \(R_3\) or of \(R_2\) and \(R_3\), which cuts the intersection of \(R_1\) and \(R_2\) at the sought point.

\(\mathrm{Op.} : (16R_1 + 8R_2 + 2C_3)\).

d) \(R_1, R_2\) intersect, \(R_3\) touches one of the first two or both.

I draw only \(\omega(\rho)\) and determine with this circumference the radical axis of two tangent circumferences:

\(\mathrm{Op.} : (8R_1 + 4R_2 + C_3)\).

e) \(R_1, R_2, R_3\) intersect two by two:

\(\mathrm{Op.} : (4R_1 + 2R_2)\).

XLIII. — Locate a point \(M\) given by its Cartesian coordinates \(x, y\) relative to two given axes \(ox, oy\).

I take \(OA = x\) on the \(x\)-axis \(\mathrm{op.} : (3C_1 + C_3)\). I take \(OB = y\) on the \(y\)-axis \(\mathrm{op.} : (3C_1 + C_3)\). I describe \(A(y)\) \(\mathrm{op.} : (C_1 + C_3)\). Then, taking \(x\) again between the compass legs, I describe \(B(x)\) \(\mathrm{op.} : (3C_1 + C_3)\). \(A(y)\) and \(B(x)\) intersect at \(M\):

\(\mathrm{Op.} : (10C_1 + 4C_3)\); simplicity 14; exactitude 10; 4 circles.

If I use two compasses, I do not need to take \(x\) again, but rather use the first compass; I thus save \(2C_1\) and have only:

\(\mathrm{Op.} : (8C_1 + 4C_3)\) [*] .

[*]

This question is one of those I have already treated (Congress of Oran, 1888, p. 92, construction XXXI, and Bulletin de la Soc. math. de France, vol. XVI, 1887–88, p. 163); yet, simple as it is, I had given too complicated a symbol, because I had adopted another usual construction, just as simple to express as this one; my attention not then being fixed as it is now on the differences existing among the various fundamental constructions, I had chosen and evaluated the first classical construction that came to mind, regarding it without examination as equivalent to the others; there are similar errors in many of the constructions I have given hitherto. Those in this memoir are the simplest that I have been able to find, but they are fixed as the simplest in fact only until geometers find preferable ones. It is a small experimental piece of work that will be done very quickly by one person or another, if the question holds interest. Two years ago, I had a rather long correspondence on this subject with M. G. Tarry, and I take this opportunity to thank him, for a great number of the simplifications I have made here were pointed out to me by him in that correspondence.

XLIV. — Locate the centers of similitude \(V\) and \(V_1\) of two circumferences \(O(R)\), \(O'(R')\).

Referring back to construction XXIX (second method), we see that these points are determined by the symbol:

f. 0049 · p. 33

\(\mathrm{Op.} : (6R_1 + 3R_2 + 4C_1 + 2C_3)\); simplicity 15; exactitude 10; 3 lines, 2 circles.

A single one of the two centers would be determined by:

\(\mathrm{Op.} : (4R_1 + 2R_2 + 4C_1 + 2C_3)\).

XLV. — Draw the four axes of similitude of three given circumferences \(O(R), O'(R'), O''(R'')\).

a) By determining the centers of similitude through the preceding construction, noting that one need only locate the four centers of similitude of \(O(R)\) and \(O''(R'')\), of \(O'(R')\) and \(O''(R'')\), and that \(O'O\) need not be drawn, one has the symbol:

\(\mathrm{Op.} : (20R_1 + 10R_2 + 10C_1 + 6C_3)\); simplicity 46; exactitude 30; 10 lines, 6 circles.

b) One can operate a little more simply.

I draw \(OO'\), \(O'O''\), \(OO''\) \(\mathrm{op.} : (6R_1 + 3R_2)\).

Through \(O''\) I draw a parallel to \(OO'\) \(\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 4C_1 + 2C_3)\); in \(O'\) and \(O''\) and in \(O\) and \(O''\) I have parallel diameters.

I thus have the four centers of similitude by \(\mathrm{op.} : (8R_1 + 4R_2)\), and the four axes then by \(\mathrm{op.} : (8R_1 + 4R_2)\),

In all: \(\mathrm{op.} : (24R_1 + 12R_2 + 4C_1 + 2C_3)\); simplicity 42; exactitude 28; 12 lines, 4 circles.

XLVI. — Two points \(A\) and \(B\) being located on a straight line, locate the harmonic conjugate \(C'\) of a given point \(C\), with respect to \(A\) and \(B\).

I draw an arbitrary straight line \(CDE\) passing through \(C\), then two arbitrary straight lines passing through \(A\): one cutting \(CD\) at \(D\), the other cutting the same line at \(E\) \(\mathrm{op.} : (3R_1 + 3R_2)\).

I draw \(DB\), \(EB\) \(\mathrm{op.} : (4R_1 + 2R_2)\).

\(DB\) cuts \(AE\) at \(F\), \(EB\) cuts \(AD\) at \(G\); I draw \(FG\) cutting \(AB\) at \(C\) [i.e., \(C'\)] \(\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2)\).

In all: \(\mathrm{op.} : (9R_1 + 6R_2)\); simplicity 15; exactitude 9; 6 lines.

We have just given, in what precedes, the principal constructions, that is to say those most often encountered in executing a solution; thus, with their symbols all calculated here, it will be easy and brief to find the total symbol of any construction; let us note that these fundamental symbols must never be used without examination; they can, most often, be simplified by the particular circumstances of the drawing one is executing, on account of lines already traced, etc.
