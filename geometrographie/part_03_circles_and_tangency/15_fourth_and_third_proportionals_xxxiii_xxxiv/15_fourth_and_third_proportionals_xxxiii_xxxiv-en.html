Problems XXXIII–XXXIV bis: Fourth & Third Proportionals¶

Émile Lemoine — La Géométrographie (English Translation, pp. 21–24, fols. 0037–0040)

XXXIII. — To construct the fourth proportional X to three given straight lines \(M, N, P\); \(X = \frac{N \cdot P}{M}\), or: To divide a length P proportionally to two given lengths M and N.

Here is the classical construction:

a) I trace two straight lines intersecting at A … \(\mathrm{op.} : (2R_2)\).

Upon one of the sides and in the same sense, I take \(AB = M\) ; \(AD = N\) ; then upon the other side \(AC = P\) … \(\mathrm{op.} : (9C_1 + 3C_3)\).

Then, through D a parallel to BC, I draw this parallel (without tracing BC) by the operation … \(\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 6C_1 + 2C_3)\).

I thus have:

\(\mathrm{Op.} : (2R_1 + 3R_2 + 15C_1 + 5C_3)\) ; simplicity 25 ; exactitude 17 ; 3 lines, 5 circles.

Let us even remark that if I had drawn BC, as all classical constructions indicate, I would have had the slightly more complicated symbol (although employing the simplified method, see XVII, to draw through a point D a parallel to a straight line BC) as follows:

\(\mathrm{Op.} : (4R_1 + 4R_2 + 13C_1 + 5C_3)\) ; simplicity 26 ; exactitude 17 ; 4 lines, 5 circles.

But there are other constructions which must be employed by preference because they are simpler.

b) I trace (fig. 4) an arbitrary straight line … \(\mathrm{op.} : (R_2)\).

I take upon this straight line \(RA = N\) ; \(RB = P\) … \(\mathrm{op.} : (5C_1 + C_2 + 2C_3)\).

I construct a circle passing through the points A and B; I construct RM which cuts at C the circle passing through A and B … \(\mathrm{op.} : (6C_1 + 4C_3)\).

I trace the straight line RCD (D upon the circle passing through A and B) … \(\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2)\).

\(\mathrm{Op.} : (2R_1 + 2R_2 + 11C_1 + C_2 + 6C_3)\) ; simplicity 22 ; exactitude 14 ; 2 lines, 6 circles.

c) I trace (fig. 5) a circumference of a radius greater than half the greatest of the three lines M, N, P … \(\mathrm{op.} : (C_3)\).

I take from an arbitrary point R of this circumference chords RA, RB, RC equal respectively to N, P, M, \(\mathrm{op.} : (8C_1 + C_2 + 3C_3)\).

f. 0038 · p. 22

A and B being on opposite sides of R, I trace AB … \(\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2)\).

I take upon the circumference passing through A, B, C, \(AE = BC\), E and C being on the same side of AB … \(\mathrm{op.} : (3C_1 + C_3)\).

I trace RE which cuts AB at H … \(\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2)\).

RH is X, for the two triangles ARH, CRB are similar, etc.

\(\mathrm{Op.} : (4R_1 + 2R_2 + 11C_1 + C_2 + 5C_3)\) ; simplicity 23 ; exactitude 16 ; 2 lines, 5 circles.

The constructions we indicate throughout this work are general, unless we warn to the contrary, that is to say that they can always be applied with any data whatsoever, and this is indispensable for the general study of the simplicity of a given question, since these are fundamental constructions from which one starts in order to establish the symbol of a construction to be effected. Thus, by constructions a, b, c, whatever M, N, P may be, the fourth proportional \(\frac{N \cdot P}{M}\) can be constructed. There are sometimes simpler constructions than those we have just given, but then they are not general; for example, to trace the fourth proportional \(\frac{N \cdot P}{M}\), one may operate thus when N and P are smaller than \(2M\) (see Journal de Vuibert, 1881-82, p. 58).

d) I trace from an arbitrary point O the circle OM; from an arbitrary point R of the circle, I trace RN which cuts OM at A … \(\mathrm{op.} : (4C_1 + C_2 + 2C_3)\).

I trace AP which cuts OM at B (R, A, B being in the same sense); I trace BP which cuts RN at A and at A.

AA is the sought fourth proportional … \(\mathrm{op.} : (5C_1 + 2C_3)\).

\(\mathrm{Op.} : (8C_1 + C_2 + 4C_3)\) ; simplicity 13 ; exactitude 9 ; 4 circles.

There are many others of the same kind (see, for example, Journal de Vuibert, 1881-82 p. 59). The latter is also indicated in Mathesis, 1892, p. 158, but without its lack of generality having been pointed out there.

It is, moreover, very interesting to know the principal non-general constructions of the fundamental problems of construction, because one must apply them on occasion, and also to know the general solutions less simple than those we give here, because, when certain lines are already traced on the figure, they can become the simplest; but before accepting them to establish the symbol of a construction, one must: for the former, examine whether the restrictive conditions they require are fulfilled; for the latter, whether their use effectively simplifies the construction.

XXXIV. — To construct the third proportional \(X = \frac{N^2}{M}\).

If, in the constructions of problem XXXIII, one supposes \(N = P\), one will have the sought construction.

Construction a) will give … \(\mathrm{op.} : (4R_1 + 4R_2 + 10C_1 + 4C_3)\) ;

Construction b) will give … \(\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + 2R_2 + 9C_1 + C_2 + 6C_3)\), by an easy modification, replacing the circle passing through A and through B by a circle tangent at A to RA, since A and B coincide.

c) will give … \(\mathrm{op.} : (4R_1 + 2R_2 + 5C_1 + C_2 + 3C_3)\).

It will suffice to take on the circle traced at the beginning of the construction, chord \(RA =\) chord \(RB = N\), A and B being taken on opposite sides of R, to take chord \(RC = M\), to trace RC which will cut AB at H, RH is the sought length [*] .

d) Non-general construction since it requires \(2N < M\); we find … \(\mathrm{op.} : (6C_1 + C_2 + 4C_3)\).

The simplest general construction I know for the third proportional \(X = \frac{N^2}{M}\), derived from XXXIII, therefore follows from c by the symbol:

\(\mathrm{Op.} : (4R_1 + 2R_2 + 5C_1 + C_2 + 3C_3)\) ; simplicity 15 ; exactitude 10 ; 2 lines, 3 circles.

If one has: \(N < 2M\), here is another very simple one:

I trace BM, B is arbitrary … \(\mathrm{op.} : (2C_1 + C_3)\).

A being arbitrary on BM, I trace AN … \(\mathrm{op.} : (2C_1 + C_2 + C_3)\).

I trace BD which cuts AN at C … \(\mathrm{op.} : (2R_1 + R_2)\), we have \(CD = X\).

\(\mathrm{Op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 4C_1 + C_2 + 2C_3)\) ; simplicity 10 ; exactitude 7 ; 1 line, 2 circles.

[*]

This construction gives the following theorem: If in a triangle ARC one draws from point A the perpendicular to the radius OR of the circle circumscribed about ARC, this perpendicular will cut the side CR at a point H and one will have \(AR^2 = RH \cdot RC\).

XXXIV bis. — In a triangle ABC, to construct the lengths:

\(\frac{b^2}{a}, \; \frac{c^2}{a}, \; \frac{c^2}{b}, \; \frac{a^2}{b}, \; \frac{a^2}{c}, \; \frac{b^2}{c}, \; \frac{bc}{a}, \; \frac{ca}{b}, \; \frac{ab}{c}\).

The construction for each of them is simpler than the general constructions XXXIII and XXXIV, because it is executed in a traced triangle.

f. 0040 · p. 24

I make the angle \(BAK = C\), K being on BC in the sense BC.

We have: \(AK = \frac{bc}{a}\), \(BK = \frac{c^2}{a}\).

\(\mathrm{Op.} : (2R_1 + R_2 + 5C_1 + 3C_3)\).

By making the angle \(CAH = B\), H being in the sense CB, one would likewise have \(AH = \frac{bc}{a} = AK\) ; \(CH = \frac{b^2}{a}\).

This construction is frequently used in the geometry of the triangle.
