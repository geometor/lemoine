Problems XXXIII–XXXIV bis: Fourth & Third Proportionals¶

Émile Lemoine — La Géométrographie (English Translation, pp. 21–24, fols. 0037–0040)

Problem XXXIII (Euclid VI.12 — Fourth Proportional :math:`X = frac{N cdot P}{M}` to Three Segments :math:`M, N, P`). Lemoine investigates four distinct methods—Thales parallels (a), power of a point via secants (b, FIG. 4), inscribed antiparallel chords in an arbitrary circle (c, FIG. 5), and Mascheronian concentric circles (d, non-general):

  • Method (a) — Classical Thales Parallels: Tracing two intersecting rays, transferring \(M, N, P\), and drawing the parallel costs \(4R_1 + 3R_2 + 11C_1 + C_2 + 6C_3\) (\(S = 25, E = 16\), 3 lines, 6 circles).

  • Method (b) — Power of a Point :math:`RA cdot RB = RC cdot RD` (FIG. 4, p. 21): Draw two intersecting lines through R (\(2R_2\)); lay off \(RA = N, RB = P\) on one line and \(RC = M\) on the other. Pass a circle through A, B, and C; its second intersection D with line RC yields \(RD = NP/M\) (left panel: external point R; right panel: internal point R, \(S = 22, E = 14\), XXXIII — Fourth Proportional to Three Given Segments):

\[\mathrm{Op.} : \left(2R_1 + 2R_2 + 11C_1 + C_2 + 6C_3\right), \qquad S = 22, \quad E = 14 \quad (2\text{ lines}, 6\text{ circles}).\]
f. 0038 · p. 22

  • Method (c) — Inscribed Circle Chords (FIG. 5, p. 22): Draw an arbitrary circle large enough to contain chords \(RA = N\), \(RB = P\), and \(RC = M\) from a point R on the circumference. Draw chord AB and chord RC meeting at H—or specifically, place \(RA = N, RB = P\) on opposite sides of R, mark \(RC = M\), and draw the parallel/antiparallel chord so \(\triangle RAH \sim \triangle RCB\), reading RH \(= NP/M\) directly with far fewer circles!

  • Method (d) — Concentric Circles (Non-General, :person:`Mascheroni <mascheroni>` § 93): Whenever \(M > \frac{1}{2}P\) (or \(2N < M\)), concentric circles deliver \(X\) by compass alone, underscoring Lemoine’s rule (p. 23) that restricted-domain tricks must never be substituted for general symbols unless their hypotheses are guaranteed!

f. 0039 · p. 23

Problem XXXIV (Euclid VI.11 — Third Proportional :math:`X = frac{N^2}{M}`). Setting \(N = P\) in XXXIII(c) yields Lemoine’s simplest general construction—take equal chords \(RA = RB = N\) on opposite sides of \(R\) on an arbitrary circle, take chord \(RC = M\), and draw \(AB\) and \(RC\) meeting at \(H\), whence \(RH = N^2/M\):

\[\mathrm{Op.} : \left(4R_1 + 2R_2 + 5C_1 + C_2 + 3C_3\right), \qquad S = 15, \quad E = 10 \quad (2\text{ lines}, 3\text{ circles}).\]

When \(N < 2M\), an even simpler two-circle construction (\(B(M)\), \(A(N)\) from \(A\) on \(B(M)\), join \(BD\) cutting \(A(N)\) at \(C\)) yields \(CD = N^2/M\) in \(2R_1 + R_2 + 4C_1 + C_2 + 2C_3\) (\(S = 10, E = 7\), 1 line, 2 circles!).

f. 0040 · p. 24

Problem XXXIV bis — Triangle Ratios :math:`frac{b^2}{a}, frac{c^2}{a}, frac{bc}{a}`, etc., Inside a Traced Triangle :math:`ABC`. Copying \(\angle BAK = C\) (with \(K\) on \(BC\)) yields simultaneously \(AK = \frac{bc}{a}\) and \(BK = \frac{c^2}{a}\) in a single angle-transfer symbol \(2R_1 + R_2 + 5C_1 + 3C_3\) (\(S = 11, E = 7\))—an indispensable engine for triangle-center Problems L–LXIII!
