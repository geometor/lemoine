Introduction: Aim & Ideal Postulates of Geometrography — Scholarly Notes¶

Archival Folio Provenance, Textual Collation & Cross-References (Intro., fols. 0017–0018)

  • Primary Witness: Émile Lemoine, La Géométrographie ou l’art des constructions géométriques, AFAS Congrès de Pau (1892), tirage à part Paris: Gauthier-Villars (1893), folios 0017–0018 (printed pp. 1–2).

  • Historical Lineage: Lemoine explicitly recalls his first public presentation at the Oran AFAS Congress (30 March 1888, lemoine-1888) and explains why he re-evaluates all elementary constructions afresh in the 1892 Pau memoir—realizing only after 1888 that nearly every classical construction taught since Euclid could be simplified, sometimes by half.

  • Philosophical Connection: Contrasts directly with Proclus’s commentary on Elements I.1–I.3 (proclus-in-euclidem) and P. H. J. Hoenen’s epistemology of constructive phantasia (hoenen-1954): Lemoine treats the instrument-hand interface itself as an exact formal calculus.

  • Cross-References: See The Five Elementary Operations & Coefficients S and E for the five elementary operations and Various Observations: Idealizations, Oran Notation & Replies to Mannheim and Brocard (pp. 52–60) for Lemoine’s extended methodological replies to Mannheim and Brocard.
