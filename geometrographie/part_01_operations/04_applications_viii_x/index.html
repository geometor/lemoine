Applications VIII–X¶

VIII. — To lay off a given length (to be taken with the compass) upon a given line from an indeterminate point of that line or from a determined point of that line:

\[\mathrm{Op.} : (2C_1 + C_2 + C_3) \quad \text{or} \quad \mathrm{op.} : (3C_1 + C_3).\]

REMARKS. — When one has to lay off the same length \(M\) \(n\) times along a straight line consecutively from A to B, from B to C, etc., the construction must be interpreted in two ways, and one will choose whichever suits the case in which one is placed.

1° The intermediate points of division are not to be marked.

We shall take \(M\) between the legs of the compass (which, in practice, will then be dividers), \(\mathrm{op.} : (2C_1)\); we shall lay off this length from A to B; we shall count \(\mathrm{op.} : (C_2 + C_1)\) or \(\mathrm{op.} : (2C_1)\) according as A is indeterminate on the straight line or determined, and not \(\mathrm{op.} : (C_2 + C_3)\) or \(\mathrm{op.} : (C_1 + C_3)\), because, leaving one point at B, we shall pass to C where we shall count \(\mathrm{op.} : (C_1)\); then leaving one point at C, we shall pass to D counting \(\mathrm{op.} : (C_1)\), etc.; we shall finally have:

\[\mathrm{Op.} : (n + 2)C_1 + C_2 \quad \text{or} \quad \mathrm{op.} : (n + 3)C_1.\]

We therefore summarize in \(\mathrm{op.} : (C_1)\) the two operations \((C_3 + C_1)\), because they are performed here at a single stroke; yet this is not quite the operation \((C_1)\) as we defined it—the assimilation seems justifiable to us in view of the question, and it avoids creating a special symbol for this particular case.

2° We mark all the intermediate points of division by transferring the dry point each time to the newly marked point, etc.; there is nothing special to say, and the symbol is:

\[\mathrm{Op.} : \left[(n + 1)C_1 + C_2 + nC_3\right] \quad \text{or} \quad \mathrm{op.} : \left[(n + 2)C_1 + nC_3\right].\]

IX. — To draw an arbitrary circle passing through two points A and B.

I describe the two circumferences AAC, BAC of the same arbitrary radius, AC being greater than \(\frac{AB}{2}\); I draw CAC \(\mathrm{op.} : (3C_1 + 3C_3)\).

X. — To place a point C at an indeterminate equal distance from two given points A and B: \(\mathrm{Op.} : (2C_1 + 2C_3)\).
