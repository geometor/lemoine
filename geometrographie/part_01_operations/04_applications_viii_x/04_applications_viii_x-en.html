Applications VIII–X: Segment Transfer & Equidistant Points¶

Émile Lemoine — La Géométrographie (English Translation, p. 6, fols. 0022)

On page 6, Lemoine completes the ten fundamental operations with segment transfer (including \(n\)-fold stepping), the arbitrary circle through two points, and the equidistant point primitive:

Application VIII — Lay Off a Given Length :math:`AB` on a Traced Line.

  • From an indeterminate point of the traced line: capture \(AB\) (\(2C_1\)), set one leg on the line (\(C_2\)), and mark the endpoint by an arc (\(C_3\)):

\[\mathrm{Op.} : \left(2C_1 + C_2 + C_3\right), \qquad S = 4, \quad E = 3 \quad (0\text{ lines}, 1\text{ circle}).\]

  • From a determined point \(M\) on the traced line: capture \(AB\) (\(2C_1\)), set the pivot at \(M\) (\(C_1\)), and sweep \(M(AB)\) (\(C_3\)): \(3C_1 + C_3\) (\(S = 4, E = 3\)).

  • Remark on :math:`n`-fold Stepping: To step a length \(AB\) \(n\) consecutive times along a traced line without marking intermediate arcs (merely pivoting the divider compass along the line) costs \((n + 2)C_1 + C_2\) (or \((n + 2)C_1\) from a fixed origin); if each intermediate division is marked with a small arc, the symbol becomes \((n + 1)C_1 + C_2 + nC_3\). Here Lemoine contrasts the modern divider compass (\(S = 4\)) directly against Euclid’s collapsing-compass transfer in Elements I.2–I.3 (\(S = 16\), VIII — Laying Off a Given Segment Along a Traced Line).

Application IX — Trace an Arbitrary Circle Passing Through Two Given Points :math:`A` and :math:`B`.

To locate an arbitrary center equidistant from \(A\) and \(B\) without drawing the perpendicular bisector, sweep two equal-radius arcs \(A(R)\) and \(B(R)\) (\(2C_1 + 2C_3\)) meeting at \(O\), then sweep \(O(R)\) (\(C_1 + C_3\)) with the same unaltered aperture \(R\):

\[\mathrm{Op.} : \left(3C_1 + 3C_3\right), \qquad S = 6, \quad E = 3 \quad (0\text{ lines}, 3\text{ circles}).\]

Application X — Place a Point :math:`C` Equidistant from Two Given Points :math:`A` and :math:`B`.

Sweep two equal-radius arcs \(A(R)\) and \(B(R)\) with arbitrary sufficient radius \(R > AB/2\); either intersection is \(C\):

\[\mathrm{Op.} : \left(2C_1 + 2C_3\right), \qquad S = 4, \quad E = 2 \quad (0\text{ lines}, 2\text{ circles}).\]

If \(C\) must lie at a prescribed distance \(l\) from both \(A\) and \(B\), capturing \(l\) adds \(2C_1\), yielding \(4C_1 + 2C_3\) (\(S = 6, E = 4\)).
