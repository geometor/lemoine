The Five Elementary Operations & Coefficients S and E¶

Émile Lemoine — La Géométrographie (English Translation, pp. 3–5, fols. 0019–0021)

Every plane construction executed with ungraduated straightedge and compass decomposes exhaustively into five—and only five—irreducible elementary operations:

  • R₁ — Bring the edge of the straightedge into coincidence with one determined point (\(\mathrm{op.} : R_1\)). Aligning the ruler simultaneously through two determined points A and B requires two point coincidences and therefore always counts as \(2R_1\).

  • R₂ — Draw the straight line along the aligned edge of the straightedge (\(\mathrm{op.} : R_2\)).

  • C₁ — Place one point of the compass upon one determined point (\(\mathrm{op.} : C_1\)). Spanning the distance AB between two placed points A and B sets both compass legs on determined points and counts as \(2C_1\). If one leg then remains pivoted at A or B to trace an arc immediately, no additional \(C_1\) is incurred; lifting the compass to place its pivot at a third determined center O adds one \(C_1\) (for \(3C_1\) in all).

  • C₂ — Place one point of the compass at an indeterminate point of a line (straight or circular) already traced (\(\mathrm{op.} : C_2\)). Setting a compass leg at an arbitrary point of the blank plane off all traced loci requires no alignment and counts as \(0\).

  • C₃ — Trace the circle or required circular arc (\(\mathrm{op.} : C_3\)), whether a brief intersecting arc or the complete circumference is drawn.

In the left panel above, aligning the ruler edge across A and B costs \(2R_1\) and drawing line AB adds \(R_2\), giving \(2R_1 + R_2\) (\(S = 3, E = 2\)). In the right panel, Lemoine's compact circle notation denotes by OAB the circle centered at O whose radius equals segment AB, while marking a length along a traced line from an indeterminate locus point M costs \(2C_1 + C_2 + C_3\) (\(S = 4, E = 3\)).

The Operation Symbol & Invariants \(S\) and \(E\)¶

If executing a complete construction requires \(l_1\) occurrences of \(R_1\), \(l_2\) of \(R_2\), \(m_1\) of \(C_1\), \(m_2\) of \(C_2\), and \(m_3\) of \(C_3\), Lemoine summarizes the entire graphical procedure by a single algebraic operation symbol:

\[\mathrm{Op.} : \left(l_1 R_1 + l_2 R_2 + m_1 C_1 + m_2 C_2 + m_3 C_3\right).\]

Four fundamental invariants are read directly from this vector:

\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} \text{Coefficient of Simplicity:} \quad S &= l_1 + l_2 + m_1 + m_2 + m_3, \\ \text{Coefficient of Exactitude:} \quad E &= l_1 + m_1 + m_2, \\ \text{Number of Traced Straight Lines:} \quad N_{\text{lines}} &= l_2, \\ \text{Number of Traced Circles:} \quad N_{\text{circles}} &= m_3. \end{aligned}\end{split}\]
f. 0021 · p. p. 5

The coefficient of simplicity \(S\) counts the total number of elementary acts, while the coefficient of exactitude \(E\) counts solely the preparatory coincidences (\(R_1, C_1, C_2\)) prior to drawing (\(R_2, C_3\)). Because every point coincidence entails a small observational inaccuracy, a smaller integer \(E\) denotes fewer opportunities for drafting error and therefore greater precision.

Lemoine also notes on pp. 4–5 (and again on pp. 55–56) why he streamlined his preliminary 1888 Oran eight-symbol system (\(\rho_1, \rho_2, \rho_3, \gamma_1, \gamma'_1, \gamma''_1, \gamma_2, \gamma'_2\) of lemoine-1888) down to \((R_1, R_2, C_1, C_2, C_3)\): distinguishing sliding vs. pivoting movements added subjective ambiguity without changing comparative rankings, whereas the five-symbol calculus yields an objective invariant verified identically by every geometer.
