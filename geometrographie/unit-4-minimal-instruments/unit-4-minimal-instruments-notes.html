Unit IV Commentary & Notes: Mohr–Mascheroni, Poncelet–Steiner & Hilbert’s Field Hierarchy¶

Historical Lineage, Projective Obstructions & Algebraic Constructibility

1. From Mohr (1672) and Mascheroni (1797) to Bonaparte and Fazzari (1901)¶

As Gaetano Fazzari documents in the historical preface to his 1901 Palermo edition of La Geometria del Compasso (fazzari-1901, pp. v–xiii), Lorenzo Mascheroni met Napoléon Bonaparte in northern Italy in 1797 and dedicated the Pavia editio princeps (mascheroni-1797) to him in Italian hendecasyllabic verse. Returning to Paris, Bonaparte famously astonished Laplace and Lagrange at the Institut National by presenting Mascheroni’s compass-only quadrisection of the circle (Libro II, § 34: stepping three hexagon chords \(B, C, D\) of radius \(r\) so \(AC = BD = r\sqrt{3}\), intersecting \(A(AC)\) and \(D(BD)\) at \(E\) so \(OE = r\sqrt{2}\), and cutting the circle from \(A\) with radius \(OE\) in just 4 auxiliary arcs), provoking Laplace’s celebrated remark: “Nous attendions tout de vous, général, excepté des leçons de géométrie.”

Only in 1928 did Danish mathematician Johannes Hjelmslev examine a student-submitted 1672 Amsterdam booklet by Georg Mohr, Euclides Danicus (mohr-1672), discovering that Mohr had solved the entire compass-only program 125 years before Mascheroni! Even more remarkably, August Adler (adler-1890) showed that circular inversion (\(5C_1 + 3C_3\), Lemoine Problem XXXVIII) provides a single uniform functor translating every straightedge step into compass arcs.

2. Poncelet–Steiner vs. Hilbert Chapter VII: Euclidean vs. Pythagorean Fields¶

Comparing Steiner’s 1833 fixed-circle constructions (steiner-1833) with David Hilbert’s 1899 Grundlagen der Geometrie Chapter VII (hilbert-1899, §§ 36–39; published in geometor/hilbert) clarifies the exact algebraic role of a traced circle locus:

  • In Hilbert §§ 36–39, the geometer possesses a straightedge and an Eichmaß (segment-transferrer capable of laying off a fixed unit length \(1\) along any ray, equivalent to Lemoine’s équerre + unit gauge without a drawn circle). Hilbert proves that this instrument constructs a point if and only if its coordinates lie in the pythagorean closure \(\Omega_{\text{pyth}}\) (closed under \(\sqrt{1 + \omega^2}\), sums of squares). Crucially, \(\sqrt{1 + \sqrt{2}} \notin \Omega_{\text{pyth}}\): not every circle–line intersection can be executed!

  • In Poncelet–Steiner (1822 / 1833), replacing the movable unit gauge by one single drawn circle \(\Omega\) with its center \(M\) upgrades constructibility from the pythagorean field \(\Omega_{\text{pyth}}\) to the full Euclidean quadratic closure \(\mathbb{E}\) (closed under \(\sqrt{\alpha}\) for every positive \(\alpha > 0\)), because Steiner’s Seventh Problem projects arbitrary secants homothethically onto the actual continuous circumference \(\Omega\)!

3. Why Géométrographie Bridges Synthetic Duality and Algorithmic Cost¶

Viewed through Lemoine’s operation vectors:

  • Mascheroni trades \((l_1, l_2) \to (0, 0)\) at the price of simulating collinear incidences via inversion/fourth-proportional circles (e.g., midpoint \(S = 7 \to 17\)); yet on metric/inversive problems (point reflection \(S = 6\), parallel point \(S = 7\), circle inversion \(S = 8\), fourth proportional \(S = 14\)), Mascheroni ties or beats standard ruler-and-compass textbooks!

  • Steiner trades \((m_1, m_2, m_3) \to (0, 0, 0)\) once fixed circle \(M\) is given; on metric transfers (Sixth–Eighth Problems) homothetic simulation increases \(S\), yet on projective/harmonic problems (polar \(12R_1 + 7R_2, S = 19\); harmonic conjugate \(9R_1 + 6R_2, S = 15\); tangents to a drawn circle \(16R_1 + 9R_2, S = 25\)), Steiner’s pure straightedge beats compass-assisted constructions!
