Problem XLIX: Constructing a Point from Normal Coordinates¶

Émile Lemoine — La Géométrographie (English Translation, pp. 40–42, fols. 0056–0058)

Problem XLIX — Locate a Point :g:`M` with Respect to Reference Triangle :g:`ABC` Given Lengths :math:`l, m, n` Proportional to Its Normal (Trilinear) Coordinates (FIG. 11). Opening his capstone investigation of modern triangle geometry (lemoine-1873, altshiller-court-1952, kimberling-etc), Lemoine first solves the universal coordinate-plotting problem: given three signed distances \(l, m, n\) proportional to the perpendicular distances from M to sides BC, CA, AB, construct M without computing algebraic coordinates!

On folios 0056–0057 (pp. 40–41, FIG. 11): 1. Erect three perpendiculars AO, BO, C to sides BC, CA, AB (conveniently as the three perpendicular bisectors of ABC via three shared-radius vertex circles: \(6R_1 + 3R_2 + 3C_1 + 3C_3\)). 2. Along each perpendicular from feet A, B, C, lay off signed normal offsets \(A'\alpha_1 = l\), \(B'\beta_1 = m\), and \(C'\gamma_1 = n\) (inward if positive, outward if negative—note γ₁ lies outward on FIG. 11!) and trace the three parallels CB \(\parallel BC\), CA \(\parallel CA\), and AB \(\parallel AB\) (each via a square corner A on BC, costing \(3[2R_1 + R_2 + 5C_1 + 3C_3]\)). 3. These three parallels form a triangle \(\triangle A_2 B_2 C_2\) directly homothetic to ABC; joining two pairs of corresponding vertices AM (through AA) and BM (through BB, \(4R_1 + 2R_2\)) fixes their center of homothety—the exact sought point M!

f. 0057 · p. 41
\[\mathrm{Op.} : \left(16R_1 + 8R_2 + 18C_1 + 12C_3\right), \qquad S = 54, \quad E = 34 \quad (8\text{ lines}, 12\text{ circles}).\]

On page 41, Lemoine improves his own 1888 Bulletin de la Soc. math. solution (\(S = 71 \to S = 54\)) and pauses for a profound epistemological reflection: there is no general theoretical criterion guaranteeing that a given construction is the absolute minimum possible, because another geometer may discover either a better graphical execution of the same theorem or a simpler underlying theorem; yet practically, recording each improvement rapidly converges to definitive minimal benchmarks.

f. 0058 · p. 42

When two absolute normal coordinates \(l, m\) are known (case b, pp. 41–42), erecting perpendiculars at \(C\) yields \(M\) in \(S = 38\) (\(12R_1 + 6R_2 + 12C_1 + 8C_3\), \(E = 24\), or \(16R_1 + 8R_2 + 9C_1 + 5C_3\), \(E = 25\)). Yet Lemoine concludes (p. 42) that generic trilinear plotting (:math:`S = 54`) is always far more complicated than exploiting the synthetic geometry of each individual triangle center—launching Problems L–LXIII!
