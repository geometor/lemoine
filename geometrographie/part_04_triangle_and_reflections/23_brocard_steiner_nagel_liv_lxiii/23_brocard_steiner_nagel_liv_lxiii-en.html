Problems LIV–LXIII: Brocard, Steiner, Tarry, Gergonne, Spieker, Nagel & Neuberg¶

Émile Lemoine — La Géométrographie (English Translation, pp. 45–52, fols. 0061–0068)

Across pages 45–52, Lemoine completes his tour of nineteenth-century triangle geometry (altshiller-court-1952, kimberling-etc), achieving dramatic operational reductions on every major remarkable point:

Problem LIV — Single :term:`Brocard Point <points-de-brocard>` :math:`omega` (:math:`angleomega AC = angleomega CB = angleomega BA`). Using Henri Brocard’s 1881 Algiers angle construction on three equal-radius vertex circles \(A(R), B(R), C(R)\):

\[\mathrm{Op.} : \left(8R_1 + 4R_2 + 10C_1 + 6C_3\right), \qquad S = 28, \quad E = 18 \quad (4\text{ lines}, 6\text{ circles}).\]
f. 0062 · p. p. 46

Problem LV — Both Brocard Points :math:`omega` and :math:`omega’` Simultaneously (:math:`S = 38` vs. Brocard’s :math:`S = 74`). Reusing the exact same circles \(A(R), B(R), C(R)\) and the Brocard-angle chord still held in the compass adds only \(4R_1 + 2R_2 + 2C_1 + 2C_3\) to locate \(\omega'\) as well (LIV–LV — First and Second Brocard Points omega and omega’ of a Triangle):

\[\mathrm{Op.} : \left(12R_1 + 6R_2 + 12C_1 + 8C_3\right), \qquad S = 38, \quad E = 24 \quad (6\text{ lines}, 8\text{ circles}),\]

nearly halving Brocard’s own letter construction (\(S = 74\), dissected on pp. 58–59)!

Problem LVI — :person:`Steiner <steiner>` Point :math:`X(99)` (via :person:`John Casey <casey>`’s *Sequel to Euclid*, 5th ed.). Sweeping \(A(BC), B(CA), C(AB)\) (\(9C_1 + 3C_3\)) meeting pairwise on the circumcircle at \(A_1, B_1, C_1\), intersecting \(B_1 C_1\) with \(BC\) at \(A_2\) and \(C_1 A_1\) with \(AC\) at \(B_2\), and joining \(AA_2\) with \(BB_2\) (\(8R_1 + 4R_2\)): \(8R_1 + 4R_2 + 9C_1 + 3C_3\) (\(S = 24, E = 17\), 4 lines, 3 circles).

Problem LVII — :person:`Gaston Tarry <tarry>` Point :math:`X(98)` (Antipodal to Steiner Point on Circumcircle). Tracing the circumcircle (\(4R_1 + 2R_2 + 5C_1 + 4C_3\)), locating Steiner point \(R\) via \(A(BC)\) and \(B_1 C_1, AA_2\), and drawing diameter \(RO\): \(10R_1 + 5R_2 + 8C_1 + 5C_3\) (\(S = 28, E = 18\), 5 lines, 5 circles).

f. 0063 · p. p. 47

Problem LVIII — Metric Lengths :math:`frac{S}{R}` and :math:`frac{2S}{R}` (Triangle Area / Circumradius). Noting that the projections \(A'_b, A'_c\) of altitude foot \(A'\) onto \(AC, AB\) satisfy \(A'_b A'_c = S/R\), reflection via XVIII bis yields \(S/R\) in \(6R_1 + 3R_2 + 12C_1 + 6C_3\) (\(S = 27, E = 18\)), while doubling via the reflections \(A'_{1c} A'_{1b} = 2S/R\) needs only 1 line and 5 circles: \(2R_1 + R_2 + 10C_1 + 5C_3\) (\(S = 18, E = 12\))!

f. 0064 · p. p. 48

Problem LIX — :person:`Gergonne <gergonne>` Point :math:`X(7)` (and All Four Gergonne Points). A single Gergonne point costs \(10R_1 + 5R_2 + 9C_1 + 3C_3\) (\(S = 27, E = 19\), 5 lines, 3 circles); all four Gergonne points together cost \(22R_1 + 11R_2 + 9C_1 + 3C_3\) (\(S = 45, E = 31\), 11 lines, 3 circles).

Problem LX — Perimeter Centroid (:person:`Spieker <spieker>` Center :math:`X(10)`). Sweeping \(B(a), A(a)\) and three further circles of radius \(a\) yields the Spieker center in \(8R_1 + 4R_2 + 10C_1 + 7C_3\) (\(S = 29, E = 18\), 4 lines, 7 circles).

f. 0065 · p. p. 49

Problem LXI — :person:`Nagel <nagel>` Point :math:`X(8)` (Trilinears :math:`frac{p - a}{a} : frac{p - b}{b} : frac{p - c}{c}`). Assuming \(a > b > c\), sweep \(A(a)\) cutting \(AC\) at \(\beta\) and \(AB\) at \(\gamma\) (\(3C_1 + C_3\)), sweep \(C(C\beta)\) cutting \(CB\) at \(\beta_1\) and \(B(B\gamma)\) cutting \(BC\) at \(\gamma_1\) (\(4C_1 + 2C_3\)), and draw \(\beta\beta_1, \gamma\gamma_1\) (\(4R_1 + 2R_2\))—meeting directly at \(X(8)\) without constructing a single excircle (LXI — Nagel Point X(8) of a Given Triangle):

\[\mathrm{Op.} : \left(4R_1 + 2R_2 + 7C_1 + 3C_3\right), \qquad S = 16, \quad E = 11 \quad (2\text{ lines}, 3\text{ circles vs. classical }S = 34).\]

Problem LXII — :person:`Neuberg <neuberg>` Radical Center :math:`Phi` (:math:`frac{a^2b^2 + a^2c^2 - b^2c^2}{a}`, etc.).

f. 0066 · p. p. 50

Constructing the two Brocardians of the Lemoine line via a rhombus parallel through \(B\) and two symmedian feet gives \(\Phi\) in \(10R_1 + 5R_2 + 15C_1 + 7C_3\) (\(S = 37, E = 25\), 5 lines, 7 circles).

Problem LXIII — Point :math:`W` (:math:`frac{a^4 - b^2 c^2}{a}`, etc., :math:`S = 148 to S = 62`).

f. 0067 · p. p. 51

Evaluating \(W\) directly via five scaled ratios from Problem XXXIV bis and trilinear plotting (XLIX) costs a staggering \(32R_1 + 16R_2 + 68C_1 + 32C_3\) (\(S = 148, E = 100\), 16 lines, 32 circles!).

f. 0068 · p. p. 52

On page 52, Lemoine slashes that figure by more than half—down to :math:`S = 62`—using a synthetic concurrency theorem for \(W\), bringing the 63-problem constructive canon to a triumphant close before turning to Observations diverses.
