Problems L–LIII: Centroid X(2), Lemoine Point X(6) & Lemoine Line¶

Émile Lemoine — La Géométrographie (English Translation, pp. 42–45, fols. 0058–0061)

Having set the generic trilinear baseline at \(S = 54\) (Problem XLIX), Lemoine turns to the foundational centers of modern triangle geometry (lemoine-1873, altshiller-court-1952; cross-referenced to Clark Kimberling’s ETC (kimberling-etc) in ):

Problem L — Centroid :g:`G` :math:`= X(2)` (Trilinears :math:`frac{1}{a} : frac{1}{b} : frac{1}{c}`). Naive trilinear plotting (constructing \(a^2/b, a^2/c\) via XXXIV + XLIX) would cost \(S = 84\)!

f. 0059 · p. 43

  • Method (a) — Two Medians via Perpendicular Bisectors: \(8R_1 + 4R_2 + 3C_1 + 3C_3\) (\(S = 18, E = 11\), 4 lines, 3 circles).

  • Method (b) — Parallelogram Shortcut (Lemoine’s Optimum, :math:`S = 17`): Sweep \(B(CA)\) and \(A(CB)\) meeting at \(C'\) (\(6C_1 + 2C_3\)), draw \(C'B\) cutting \(B(CA)\) again at \(A'\) (\(2R_1 + R_2\)), and intersect medians \(CC'\) and \(AA'\) (\(4R_1 + 2R_2\)) at G (L — Centroid G = X(2) of a Given Triangle):

\[\mathrm{Op.} : \left(6R_1 + 3R_2 + 6C_1 + 2C_3\right), \qquad S = 17, \quad E = 12 \quad (3\text{ lines}, 2\text{ circles}).\]

Problem LI — :term:`Lemoine Symmedian Point <point-de-lemoine>` :g:`K` :math:`= X(6)` (Trilinears :math:`a : b : c`, :cite:`lemoine-1873`). Directly from Problem XLIX, trilinears \(a : b : c\) cost \(S = 54\). Lemoine cuts the cost exactly in half using his antiparallel/rhombus symmedian construction (drawing only two straight lines—the two symmedians AK and BK!): 1. On AC mark \(AC' = AB\) via \(A(AB)\) (\(2C_1 + C_3\)), and keeping the compass pivot at A, mark \(AB' = AC\) on AB (\(C_1 + C_3\)), creating an antiparallel segment PaQa (\(B'C'\)). 2. Sweep \(C'(AC)\) (\(C_1 + C_3\), radius already in compass!) and \(B'(AB)\) (\(3C_1 + C_3\)) meeting at \(A'\); draw symmedian AK = \(AA'\) (\(2R_1 + R_2\), costing \(2R_1 + R_2 + 7C_1 + 4C_3\), \(S = 14\) for a single symmedian!). 3. Construct the second symmedian BK saving one \(C_1\) (\(2R_1 + R_2 + 6C_1 + 4C_3\), since radius \(AB\) is already held!):

\[\mathrm{Op.} : \left(4R_1 + 2R_2 + 13C_1 + 8C_3\right), \qquad S = 27, \quad E = 17 \quad (2\text{ lines}, 8\text{ circles}).\]

With two compasses, Lemoine saves \(4C_1 + C_3\) to reach \(S = 22, E = 13\) (2 lines, 7 circles, LI — Lemoine Symmedian Point K = X(6) of a Given Triangle)!

f. 0060 · p. 44

Problem LII — :term:`Lemoine Line <droite-de-lemoine>` (Trilinear Polar of :math:`X(6)`). Constructing the intersections of two sides of ABC with the circumcircle tangents at their opposite vertices (via Problem XXXIV bis external angle copying) draws the Lemoine line in \(6R_1 + 3R_2 + 6C_1 + 4C_3\) (\(S = 19, E = 12\), 3 lines, 4 circles).

Problem LIII — Simultaneous Construction of Centroid :g:`G` :math:`= X(2)` and Lemoine Point :g:`K` :math:`= X(6)`.

f. 0061 · p. 45

Sweeping three equal-radius circles \(A(R), B(R), C(R)\) (\(3C_1 + 3C_3\)) yields both the midpoints \(A', B'\) (for medians \(AA', BB'\) meeting at G) and the equal-radius circular arcs inside angles \(A, B\) across which reflecting the medians directly gives the isogonal conjugate symmedians meeting at K:

\[\mathrm{Op.} : \left(12R_1 + 6R_2 + 9C_1 + 5C_3\right), \qquad S = 32, \quad E = 21 \quad (6\text{ lines}, 5\text{ circles}),\]

saving 12 full operations over building G (\(S = 17\)) and K (\(S = 27\)) separately (\(17 + 27 = 44\))!
