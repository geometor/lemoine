Various Observations: Idealizations, Oran Notation & Replies to Mannheim and Brocard — Scholarly Notes¶

Archival Folio Provenance, Textual Collation & Cross-References (Observations, fols. 0068–0076)

  • Primary Witness: Folios 0068–0076 (printed pp. 52–60).

  • Textual Collation & 1893 Print Variants: * p. 54 footnote: Lemoine poses the open probabilistic question of the ratio \(E_n / E_{n'}\) of probable final errors between two constructions of simplicities \(n\) and \(n'\). * p. 55 footnote: Notes exclusion of the équerre from high-precision constructions while referencing the 1888 Oran Congress (lemoine-1888, p. 9) for its symbolic treatment. * p. 58: The 1893 print reads 5 droites, 3 cercles beside \(\mathrm{Op.} : (10R_1 + 5R_2 + 2C_3)\) (Problem XLI uses 2 circles; 3 is a print misprint), and cites (construction LII) where Mannheim’s Lemoine-point construction corresponds to Problem LI (\(K = X(6)\)). * p. 59: Step 5° prints \(2R_1 + 2R_2 + 3C_1 + C_3\) (typographical slip for \(2R_1 + R_2 + 3C_1 + C_3\), since the summed total on the same page is \(22R_1 + 11R_2 + 24C_1 + 17C_3\)). * p. 60: Item 2° prints statistique graphique for statique graphique (graphical statics).

  • Historical Significance: The debates with Mannheim and Brocard converted Géométrographie from an abstract curiosity into an active international research program—immediately inspiring John Sturgeon Mackay’s 1893 Edinburgh study of Euclid’s Elements (mackay-1893), August Adler’s restricted-instrument synthesis (adler-1890), and Gaetano Fazzari’s 1901 Mascheroni edition (fazzari-1901).
